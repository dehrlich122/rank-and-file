# M3.4 · Chapter 4, Eyes Open: level specs

Branch `m3-4-chapter-4`. This is **checkpoint 1**: the designer approves
these specs before the levels are built. They build on the decisions in
`docs/M3.md` → Chapter 4 *(Designer, 2026-10-01)*.

The specs give each level's concept, map, rules, par and boards. They never
give solution code. References, wrong attempts, hints and solution notes
are written blind when the levels are built. Every board below was checked
with the real engine:
- each board can be solved on its own;
- no board's plain-call solution works on every board, so the code has to
  look and decide.

Par is provisional until the reference solution is written.

## Already built, before the specs

The M3 plan has engine features first. These are on the branch, with tests:

- **`pawn.look()`** (`7d0b862`): a word for what's on the square ahead, or
  None if it's empty. `look("left")` and `look("right")` see the two
  squares diagonally ahead. It costs no tick and has a Codex entry. The
  goal and waypoints look empty, so a hidden goal stays hidden.
- **Still rooks and bishops** (`7d0b862`): stepping onto a square they
  attack loses the run ("The rook on c3 took your pawn on b3."). Taking a
  piece ends its attacks, and a level can't start the pawn on an attacked
  square.
- **Each board can have its own enemies** (`518114f`), so a rook can stand
  somewhere else on each board.
- **A yes/no guard** (`518114f`): to a guard whose answer is True or False,
  the other one gets "Wrong!".

Still to build: drawing the pieces and their attacked squares (UI), and a
blind generator for programs with conditions (see "Engine and tools").

## What Chapter 4 teaches, and what makes each part necessary

| Level | Trains | What makes it necessary |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 True or False | True and False, comparisons, `and`/`or`/`not` | two guards who want a True or False about the hall, which differs on all four boards |
| 4.2 Mind the Gap | `if`, and `look()` | pits that are there on some boards and not on others; bridging where there's no pit is an error |
| 4.3 Road Closed | `if`/`else`, None and truthiness | the way straight on is clear on one board and blocked on the others, where the way round is the only way |
| 4.4 What Lies Ahead | `elif` | a pit, a wall or nothing: three boards, three different things to do |
| 4.5 Under Attack | rooks and bishops, `look("left")` and `look("right")` | a rook has taken the goal, on one capture square or the other, or not at all |
| 4.6 Pawn Storm *(mastery, optional)* | everything above, inside a `for` loop | rooks on either side, in different places on every board, and a line limit |

Two facts about the pawn shape the whole chapter:

- **The pawn only sees the squares next to it.** So every decision is made
  where the difference is right in front of it. No level needs turning
  just to look.
- **A pawn can take a rook, but never a bishop.** A pawn captures
  diagonally, and a bishop attacks its diagonals, so the square next to it
  is already deadly. So bishops shape routes rather than decisions (open
  question 1).

## Proposed changes to the outline (for approval)

1. **`and`, `or` and `not` join 4.1, with comparisons.** Booleans are
   where they belong, and a guard's question can make them necessary.
   Nothing makes them necessary in a later level: an ordered `elif` chain
   can always avoid them.
2. **4.1 asks, rather than printing and comparing.** The outline had
   "`at_goal()` and `look()` answers, printed and compared". A guard who
   wants True or False (a second guard for the compound question) makes
   the comparison necessary. `at_goal()` is unlocked from here and shown
   in the lesson.
3. **None and truthiness move to 4.3**, where `if pawn.look():` asks "is
   anything there?". Comparing numbers moves to 4.1.
4. **4.4 is three things, three actions:** a pit (bridge it), a wall (go
   round) or nothing (walk on). The outline had "three possibilities"
   with counts compared.
5. **4.5: a rook on the goal.** On each board it stands on one of the two
   capture squares, or the goal is free.
6. **The mastery is a pawn storm:** the same decision as 4.5, every round
   of a loop, up a corridor of rooks.

## The levels

In every map `P` is the start and `G` the goal, `#` is a wall and `O` a
pit. The pawn starts facing north. Enemies are listed under each map,
since they aren't drawn in the map text.

### 4.1 · True or False

- **Trains:** True and False, comparisons (`>`, `<`, `==`, `!=`, `>=`,
  `<=`), and `and`, `or` and `not`.
- **Brief:** "Two guards stand between you and the goal. Each asks a
  question about the hall you came up, and wants a plain True or False."
- **Boards** (four; this is board 4, 7×9; boards 1 to 3 have halls of 2, 4
  and 5 squares):
  ```
  # # # # # # #
  . . X . Y G #
  . # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #
  P # # # # # #
  ```
- **The guards:**
  - `X`: "Halt! Is the hall you came up longer than 4 squares? Answer True
    or False."
  - `Y`: "And was it shorter than 3 squares, or longer than 6? Answer True
    or False."
  - Their answers differ, so the four boards cover all four combinations.
    A hall of 4 checks `>` against `>=`.
- **Abilities:** `move`, `turn_left`, `turn_right`, `squares_ahead`,
  `wait`, and `at_goal` (new here).
- **Rules:** none; the boards do the forcing.
- **Par:** 8.
- **Must fail:**
  - board 1's answers typed in (wrong on the others)
  - one guard's comparison used for both
- **Lesson:**
  - `print(pawn.at_goal())`, and comparisons printed: True and False are
    values.
  - `and`, `or` and `not` on comparisons.
  - On a lesson board with a yes/no guard: a wrong answer gets "Wrong!",
    then a comparison opens the gate.

### 4.2 · Mind the Gap

- **Trains:** `if`: lines that run only when a condition is True. And
  `pawn.look()`.
- **Brief:** "The hall has two places where the floor may have fallen away.
  On each board it's different. You're carrying two planks."
- **Boards** (3×9; pits on b3 and b6 on board 1, only b3 on board 2, only
  b6 on board 3):
  ```
  board 1    board 2    board 3
  # G #      # G #      # G #
  # . #      # . #      # . #
  # . #      # . #      # . #
  # O #      # . #      # O #
  # . #      # . #      # . #
  # . #      # . #      # . #
  # O #      # O #      # . #
  # . #      # . #      # . #
  # P #      # P #      # P #
  ```
- **Abilities:** as 4.1, plus `look` and `bridge` (new here). The pawn
  starts with 2 planks.
- **Rules:** none.
- **Par:** 7.
- **Must fail:**
  - bridging every spot (a `BridgeError` where there's no pit)
  - walking straight on (falls into a pit)
- **Lesson** (on a lesson board with a pit and a plank):
  - `print(pawn.look())` facing a wall, a pit, and nothing: a word, or
    None.
  - An `if` with its indented lines, which bridge only when `look()` sees
    a pit. Walking over a plank picks it up.
  - A `run lost` snippet: walking on without looking.

### 4.3 · Road Closed

- **Trains:** `if`/`else`: one thing or the other. None counts as False,
  so `if pawn.look():` asks "is anything there?". Also `not`.
- **Brief:** "The road north is closed on some boards, by a wall or a
  signpost. Where it's closed, go the long way round. Where it's open, the
  long way is shut."
- **Boards** (5×5):
  ```
  board 1      board 2      board 3
  # G . . .    # G . . .    # G . . .
  # . # # .    # . # # .    # . # # .
  # . # # #    # # # # .    # S # # .
  # . . . .    # . . . .    # . . . .
  # P # # #    # P # # #    # P # # #
  ```
  - `S`: "No entry. By order of the management."
- **Abilities:** as 4.2.
- **Rules:** none.
- **Par:** 10.
- **Must fail:**
  - always the long way (shut on board 1)
  - always straight on (bumps on boards 2 and 3)
- **Lesson:**
  - `else` after `if`, on a lesson board where the way ahead is open or
    not.
  - `if pawn.look():` and `if not pawn.look():`: None counts as False,
    and any word as True.

### 4.4 · What Lies Ahead

- **Trains:** `elif`: a chain of conditions, where the first that's True
  wins.
- **Brief:** "Something different lies across the road on every board: a
  pit, a wall, or nothing at all. You're carrying one plank."
- **Boards** (5×5):
  ```
  board 1      board 2      board 3
  . . . G #    . . . G #    . . . G #
  . # # . #    . # # . #    . # # . #
  # # # O #    . # # # #    # # # . #
  . . . . #    . . . . #    . . . . #
  # # # P #    # # # P #    # # # P #
  ```
  The way round is open only on board 2.
- **Abilities:** as 4.2. The pawn starts with 1 plank.
- **Rules:** none.
- **Par:** 12.
- **Must fail:**
  - 4.3's two-way choice: on board 1 it goes round, where the way round is
    shut
  - bridging without looking (a `BridgeError` on boards 2 and 3)
- **Lesson:** an `if`/`elif`/`else` chain that prints which of three things
  is ahead, on a lesson board with something different in each direction.

### 4.5 · Under Attack

- **Trains:** enemy chess pieces: rooks, which can be taken, and bishops,
  which can't. Also `look("left")` and `look("right")`.
- **Brief:** "An enemy rook has taken the goal square. On each board it
  stands somewhere else, and on one it has gone. Take it back."
- **Boards** (5×5):
  ```
  board 1      board 2      board 3
  # G . . #    # . . G #    # . G . #
  # . . . #    # . . . #    # . . . #
  # # . # #    # # . # #    # # . # #
  # # . # #    # # . # #    # # . # #
  # # P # #    # # P # #    # # P # #
  ```
  - Board 1: a rook on b5, on the goal. Board 2: a rook on d5, on the
    goal. Board 3: no rook.
  - With a rook there, the square straight ahead of c4 is attacked: walking
    on is taken.
- **Abilities:** as 4.2, plus `capture_left` and `capture_right`.
- **Rules:** none.
- **Par:** 7.
- **Must fail:**
  - capturing on the left every time (a `CaptureError` on board 2)
  - walking straight on (taken by the rook on boards 1 and 2)
- **Lesson** (on a lesson board with a rook and a bishop, their attacked
  squares drawn):
  - `look("left")` and `look("right")`, and taking the rook.
  - A `run lost` snippet: stepping onto the bishop's diagonal.

### 4.6 · Pawn Storm *(mastery, optional)*

- **Trains:** Chapter 4 mastery: conditions inside a `for` loop, on boards
  that each place their rooks differently.
- **Brief:** "A corridor full of rooks, a different storm on every board.
  Every time one stands diagonally ahead, take it before it takes you."
- **Boards** (5×8; a wall below each rook stops its file):
  ```
  board 1      board 2      board 3
  # . . G #    # G . . #    # G . . #
  # . . . #    # . . . #    # # . . #
  # . . # #    # # . . #    # . . . #
  # . . . #    # . . . #    # . # . #
  # . # . #    # . # . #    # . . . #
  # . . . #    # . . . #    # # . . #
  # # . . #    # . . # #    # . . . #
  # . P . #    # . P . #    # . P . #
  ```
  - Board 1: rooks on b3, c5 and d7. Board 2: rooks on d3, c5 and b7.
    Board 3: rooks on b4, c6 and b8 (on the goal).
  - On every board the pawn climbs one rank a round, by stepping or by
    taking, so all three take 7 rounds.
- **Abilities:** as 4.5.
- **Rules:** at most 9 lines of code.
- **Par:** 7.
- **Must fail:**
  - board 1's moves copied out (wrong on the other boards)
  - 4.5's decision written out seven times (over the line limit)
- **Lesson:** short. A question asked inside a loop gets a new answer every
  round (as in 3.2's crooked stair), so an `if` inside a loop can do
  something different each time.

## Open questions

1. **Bishops.** A pawn can't sense a bishop from a distance, and never
   takes one, so in this chapter bishops shape routes, not decisions.
   - **Recommended:** bishops appear in 4.5's lesson, where stepping onto
     a diagonal loses, and return with the Bishop tier.
   - Alternative: a bishop on a level's approach, whose diagonals the route
     has to go around. The board shows them; the code doesn't decide
     anything.
   - Alternative: a way to sense attacked squares, e.g. `look()` saying
     "attacked" for an empty square under attack. That's a new decision,
     and it would suit Chapter 5's `while`.
2. **Titles and briefs,** as written above, or changed.
