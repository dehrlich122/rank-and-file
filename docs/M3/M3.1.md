# M3.1 · The obstacle toolkit: design

Branch `m3-1-toolkit`. This is **checkpoint 1**: the designer approves this
design and the practice levels before anything is built. The decisions it
builds on are in `docs/M3/M3.md` → "Obstacles and threats".

## What M3.1 adds

| Element | On the board | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Pit** | a dark hole | Stepping in is an instant loss: "Fell into the pit on c3." |
| **Waypoint** | a ring; ✓ once crossed | Must be crossed at some point before the goal. You don't stop on it, just pass over it. |
| **Gem** | a small gem | Collected when the pawn passes over it. "Collect all the gems" can be an objective. |
| **Timed gate** | bars, with an "every 3" badge | Open only on every Nth tick; a closed one blocks like a wall. |
| **Guard with a question** | the gate from QA-002, with its question shown | Opens when the pawn says the right answer, e.g. "How many gems did you collect?" The answer is never shown. |
| **Patrol** | an enemy piece, its route drawn dotted | Walks its route one square per tick, back and forth or in a loop. |
| **Chaser** | an enemy piece, marked "chases" | Steps one square toward the pawn each tick, by a stated rule. |
| **Clockwork** | a gear badge on a patrol or chaser | Its ticks come from the code, not from the pawn. |
| **Capture** | the diagonal squares a pawn could take, when an enemy is there | `capture_left()` / `capture_right()` take an enemy diagonally forward, chess style. Armoured enemies can't be taken. |

**Being caught.** A patrol or chaser that shares a square with the pawn, or
swaps squares with it, ends the run: "Caught by the patrol on d4."

**The "lost" outcome.** Being caught and falling into a pit both give a new
outcome, **lost**. It's a final result, like "solved" or "incomplete", and it
can be replayed like any run. Under the hood it stops the program the way the
line budget does, so an `except Exception:` in player code can't swallow it.

## The clock

Every moving obstacle, and every timed gate, has a clock:

- **`action`** (the default): one tick for each square the pawn moves and
  each turn, like chess (you move, then they move). The pawn acts first, then
  every obstacle takes its step, then the game checks for a catch.
- **`line`** (clockwork): one tick each time a line of the player's code
  runs.
- **`new_line`** (clockwork): one tick only the first time each line runs.
  Hand-copied lines give an obstacle many ticks; a loop gives it a few.

Asking the board (`at_goal()`, and later `look()`) is not an action and
costs no tick.

## Level files

```yaml
map: |
  # # # # # # #
  # . . O . G #
  # . # # # . #
  # P . W . . #
  # # # # # # #
legend:
  O: {tile: pit}
  W: {tile: waypoint}
  $: {tile: gem}
  T: {tile: timed_gate, every: 3}          # open when the tick count is a multiple of 3
  X: {tile: gate, question: "How many gems did you collect?", passphrase: "4"}
enemies:
  - {kind: patrol, start: b4, route: [b4, e4], clock: action}                # back and forth
  - {kind: patrol, start: c2, route: [c2, f2, f5, c5], loop: true}           # round and round
  - {kind: chaser, start: g6, clock: new_line, strategy: simple}
objectives:
  - reach_goal
  - waypoints            # added automatically when the map has any
  - collect: all         # or a number
```

- Squares are written as chess names (`b4`) in level files, like everything
  the player reads.
- Routes list their corners; patrols walk the straight lines between them.
- The level checker validates everything:
  - routes stay on floor squares
  - enemies don't start on the pawn
  - timed gates have `every` of 2 or more
  - guards with a question have an answer
- Every event carries the whole world's state, as today. It gains:
  - the enemies' positions
  - the gems collected
  - the waypoints crossed
  - the tick count

  So playback can draw any moment directly, and step-back and scrubbing stay
  free.

## What the player sees

- **Before running:**
  - pits, waypoints and gems
  - each patrol's dotted route
  - each chaser's "chases" mark
  - clockwork gear badges
  - timed gates' "every N" badges
- **In the Challenge panel:** an **Obstacles** section says each one's rule in
  words, worded by the engine like the goals and rules. For example: "The
  patrol walks b4 to e4 and back, one square each time you move or turn."
- **During playback:** obstacles move with their ticks, waypoints tick off,
  gems disappear, and timed gates open and close.
- **On a loss:** the outcome card shows **Lost** and where it happened, and
  the board marks the square.

the UI.

   "lost" outcome, pits.
2. **Waypoints, gems** and their objectives.
3. **Timed gates, guards with a question.**
4. **Patrols and chasers,** with the tracer feeding the `line` and

## Practice levels (Testing ground)

One short level per mechanic, so the designer can play each one before it's
placed in a chapter. Each has a short lesson about its mechanic.

| Level | Mechanic | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| Stepping Stones | pits | cross a field of pits without a misstep |
| Checkpoints | waypoints | cross three marked squares on the way to the goal |
| The Sentry's Round | a patrol (`action` clock) | time your moves to slip past its route |
| Pursuit | a chaser (`action` clock) | lead it around a wall and reach the goal first |
| Clockwork | a clockwork patrol (`new_line` clock) | reach the goal before it closes the way |
| Portcullis | a timed gate | arrive while it's open |
| The Toll | gems and a guard with a question | collect the gems, then answer the guard |
| The Capture | capturing (a patrol, and an armoured one) | take the patrol that can be taken, and steer clear of the armoured one |

## Decided *(Designer, 2026-09-26)*

- **One tick per square.** With the `action` clock, `pawn.move(3)` walks three
  squares and obstacles move after each one, so it plays out exactly like
  three `move()` calls. A long move can't slip past a patrol.
- **`pawn.wait()`** is a new ability, locked unless a level unlocks it. It
  spends one tick standing still, for timing puzzles: letting a patrol pass,
  or waiting for a timed gate to open.
- **Chasers start simple and predictable.** Each tick a chaser steps one
  square toward the pawn along the direction with the bigger gap. If that's
  blocked it tries the other, and if both are blocked it waits. You can work
  its moves out by hand, and walls can trap it.
  - **More cunning chasers come later in the curriculum,** e.g. one that
    finds the shortest way around walls.
  - A chaser's `strategy` is part of the level file (`simple` for now), so
    a cunning kind can be added later without changing existing levels.
- **Gems are collected by passing over them,** like crossing a waypoint.
  `pick_up()` stays in the brief's ability list for a later chapter that needs
  a deliberate action.
- **The pawn can capture, chess style** *(Designer, 2026-09-26)*:
  - `pawn.capture_left()` and `pawn.capture_right()` take an enemy standing
    diagonally forward, and the pawn moves onto its square, as in chess. The
    ability names are settled when it's built.
  - A capture is an action, so it costs one tick, like a move.
  - Moving into an enemy any other way is still being caught.
  - Capturing an empty square is an error, like bumping a wall ("There's
    nothing to capture on e5"). That's what makes checking the board first
    worthwhile.
  - Levels can mark an enemy as **armoured** (it can't be taken). "Capture
    every enemy" can be an objective (`capture: all`).
  - **Built now, in M3.1,** with its own practice level. Curriculum levels
    first use it in **Chapter 4**, where checking the board first is taught.
    Promoted pieces capture their own way later (a knight's jump, a rook's
    line).

## Built *(2026-09-27)*

All seven steps are on `m3-1-toolkit`. Details settled while building:

- **A chaser's tie goes east or west.** When the gaps along the rank and the
  file are equal, a simple chaser steps along the rank first.
- **Timed gates start open.** "Every 3" means open on ticks 0, 3, 6, …; the
  Obstacles text says so.
- **Code clocks tick as a line starts,** so an enemy on the `line` or
  `new_line` clock moves before that line's own moves.
- **The abilities are `pawn.wait()`, `pawn.capture_left()` and
  `pawn.capture_right()`.** `wait()` takes no number. (Changed in M3.2:
  `wait(ticks)` takes an optional number of ticks, 0 or more, because
  Chapter 2 computes a wait before loops arrive.)
- **A capture that misses is a `CaptureError`**, with the same bump
  animation as a wall.
- **Practice levels are `practice-02` to `practice-09`.** Their maps were
  checked with `scripts/solve.py` so each has a solution and the obvious
  routes fail. Their third hints (a partial example) and solution notes'
  line counts come from the generated solutions. The first two hints are
  general nudges about each level's mechanic.
  - After play-testing (QA-025 to QA-028), the search prefers `wait()` to
    turning on the spot, Pursuit unlocks `wait()`, and Stepping Stones'
    hint 3 shows how to bridge rather than where the route starts.
- **Not built:** a tick counter on the board. The Obstacles text and
  playback show the timing; add one if play-testing shows it's needed.

### Pits, reworked after play-testing (QA-017, 2026-09-27)

In play, a pit was just a wall with a different message. The designer chose
two ways to set pits apart:

- **Pits swallow enemies.** A chaser doesn't avoid pits. If its step lands
  on one, it falls in and is gone. A wall only holds a chaser; a pit gets
  rid of it.
- **Planks bridge pits.** Planks are picked up by walking over them, or
  handed out at the start (`start: {planks: 1}`). `pawn.bridge()` lays one
  over the pit ahead. Walls can't be bridged.

Stepping Stones was reworked around a plank. A new practice level,
**Pitfall** (`practice-10`), is about luring a chaser into a pit.
