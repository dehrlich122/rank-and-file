# M3.7 · Look & Feel: plan and step 0 (direction)

The first major design pass (`DESIGN.md` §9, with §8's promotion ceremony and
knight skin). It runs in the five steps §9 sets out, with the designer choosing
at the end of step 0. The brief and every decision it holds are in `DESIGN.md`
§9, "Decided for the pass" and "Step 0 feedback". This file holds what was built
to choose a direction (two rounds), the voice guide, and two proposals.

## Step 0, round 3 *(Designer, 2026-10-02)*

Neither round 2 style landed; the noir tile is parked as it is (not edited).
The feedback is in `DESIGN.md` §9, "Step 0 feedback": vector beat pixel
(bigger models, distinct silhouettes, characters rather than icons); pieces must
pop off the board with better shading and perspective while filling most of
their square; the hatch can be much quieter; amber worked; the goal needs to be
more eye-catching.

Before anything is built out, four quite different directions, each tiny:
a 3×3 crop of the real lit-grid board (dark theme only) with the hero pawn, an
enemy rook, a wall and one threatened square, plus the pawn alone, large. No
title screen, Lessons, light theme, other tiles, animation or readability shots.

| | Direction | How the pieces are built |
|---|---|---|
| A | Wireframe | glowing line art, no fills: a 3/4-view wire mesh, back edges dimmed, a lit pad on the floor |
| B | Low-poly solid | chunky flat-shaded faces with real form, one strong light from the upper left, a dark outline and a cast shadow |
| C | Neon poster | flat bold shapes, thick ink outlines, hard cel shading with halftone, a hard offset print shadow |
| D | Hologram | translucent light, brighter at the edges, scanlines and data glyphs inside, a slight chromatic fringe, a projector pad |

A, B and D are turned on a small lathe (`design/directions/mesh.ts`), so they
share proportions but are drawn differently; C is flat by design
(`design/directions/looks.ts`). Each direction marks its enemy's corruption in
its own medium (an out-of-register ghost, a slipped band of faces, a torn
misprinted strip, a stronger fringe). Each keeps the hatch quiet in its own way
(thin lines, or halftone dots for C). The facing pointer is hidden here: in a
3/4 view the model itself could show which way it faces, a step 1 question.

Page: `/design/directions.html`. Screenshot:
`docs/M3/M3.7/shots/round3-directions.png`.

## Step 0, round 2 *(after the designer's feedback, 2026-10-02)*

**The feedback** (recorded in `DESIGN.md` §9, "Step 0 feedback"): none of the
three tiles was right; neon was the favourite. Push it toward 80s cyberpunk and
retro-futurism (Hotline Miami without the violence, *Hackers*, *The Lawnmower
Man*), less toward old video games. On the title screen, a line of enemy chess
pieces in front of the sun, and the player's hero (at the rank they've reached)
under the menu. Two follow-up choices *(Designer, 2026-10-02)*:
- **Pink in the big chrome moments only.** The title screen and the
  level-complete flourish may use the whole sunset; wherever the board can be
  seen, magenta still means hostile.
- **Compare pixel and vector sprites** on the same tile before choosing.

**What was built:** tile 4, "Neon noir", at
`/design/tiles.html?tile=noir&sprites=vector` (or `&sprites=pixel`). The Style
row on the page switches the sprites and the Lessons layout; "piece: pawn"
switches the hero on the board and on the title screen.

- **The title screen** comes first on the page. A chrome "RANK & FILE" with a
  two-step hard shadow (pink, then violet: offsets, not glow), gently swaying.
  A sunset sky that slowly shifts hue, faint scanlines on the sky only, and a
  VHS tracking band rolling down it now and then. A striped sun, with **a back
  rank of enemy rooks and bishops** silhouetted on the horizon in front of it,
  each lit along its top edge by the sun and glitching in turn. A scrolling
  cyan grid floor, the menu on its own plate, and **the hero standing on the
  grid under the menu** with its idle. With reduced motion, the sway, the sky,
  the floor, the VHS band, the blink and the glitches all stop (the enemies keep
  their static fringe).
- **Vector sprites** (`design/vector.ts`): each piece and tile is a list of
  flat-shaded polygon faces, lit from the upper left, with a thin lit edge and a
  dark outline under it, in the same colour tokens as the pixel sprites. The
  pawn is an armoured figure with a faceted helm, a lit visor and a lit core;
  the knight a faceted horse-head helm; enemies have cracks, a dead pixel and a
  lit red "eye" (the rook's arrow-slit, the bishop's cut); the chaser is a
  faceted virus. Walls are bevelled blocks with circuit traces, the pit is a
  wireframe funnel, the rune shows `>_`.
- **The waypoint is now a diamond** in both sprite styles, so it can't be
  mistaken for the goal's ring or a hidden-goal spot (a weakness from round 1).
- **Type:** Orbitron (OFL, self-hosted) for the logo, menu and headings; code
  stays monospace and lessons stay in the UI face.
- **The level-complete flourish:** a sunset banner with "RUN COMPLETE" in the
  chrome lettering slides in once, above the plain green card the game already
  shows. The card never turns pink.
- **Lessons:** the explorer with the detail pane by default (the hybrid), with
  the explorer alone one click away.
- The mood board is rebuilt around the new references (twelve entries, plus two
  kept for readability: Into the Breach and TIS-100/EXAPUNKS). The round 1
  game references are listed as dialled down.

**New files:** `design/vector.ts` (the vector sprites), `design/paint.ts` (one
interface over pixel and vector drawing, used by the board, the sprite sheet,
the glitch and the title screen), `design/noir.css` (tile 4).

**Screenshots** (`docs/M3/M3.7/shots/`): for `noir-pixel` and `noir-vector`,
the same set as round 1 (`-normal`, `-greyscale`, `-min-size-greyscale-dark`
and `-light`, `-reduced-motion`, `-glitch-held`) plus `-title-dark` and
`-title-light`. The round 1 shots were retaken after the drawing code was
shared; the only visible change is that enemy badges ("chases") now draw over
the glitch instead of under it.

**What the checks found:**
- At 20px in greyscale every vector piece and tile is told apart by shape,
  including the bishop, which was the weakest piece in round 1.
- As silhouettes on the title screen, the vector rooks and bishops read at a
  glance; the pixel ones blur into blobs. On the board the pixel set still
  works, but the vector set is clearer.
- The first vector walls were the brightest large shapes on the board, so they
  were darkened to let the pieces lead; the first pit was too faint on dark
  squares and got a lit rim.
- The noir dark theme's first square step was 1.32:1 (inside the band, below the
  brief's 1.4); the light squares were raised to 1.42:1.
- The contrast check now covers tile 4 and the title menu on its see-through
  plate (tested over the brightest sky and over the floor): 260 checks, none
  failing. The tightest margins in tile 4 are the square steps (1.42 dark, 1.44
  light) and, as in round 1, the hero against the enemy in light greyscale
  (1.37:1, told apart by shape as well).

**For the designer: what to look at**
1. **The title screen**, vector against pixel: the enemy rank before the sun,
   the hero under the menu, the arrow keys and Enter, and "piece: knight" to see
   the promoted hero.
2. **The board and the sprite sheet**, vector against pixel, in both themes.
3. **`noir-vector-min-size-greyscale-*.png`**: can you tell everything apart?
4. **The level-complete flourish** (the "error flicker + level complete" button
   replays it), and whether the pink stays where it should.
5. **The light theme's title screen**, a pastel daylight sunset.

**Open questions, round 2**
- **Pixel or vector?** My recommendation is vector: it reads better as a
  silhouette, at 20px and in greyscale, and it suits the new references.
- **The menu's "coming soon" entries** are still selectable with the arrows
  (round 1's question stands).
- **The hue shift in the sky** moves the sunset toward violet and back over 22
  seconds. Too much, or not enough?
- **Orbitron** for the chrome, or try another 80s face (a chrome logo face, or
  a condensed italic closer to Hotline Miami)?

### What the readability check found

The first drafts failed in ways worth recording:

- **Light theme sprites were too pale.** The hero was 1.13:1 against the dark
  square. In the light theme the sprites' tones now run darker, so a body reads
  against both squares.
- **Enemies were bright pink, not "magenta on a dark body".** The body is now a
  mid magenta with a bright left rim and a dark shade, and cracks and dead pixels
  on top. Making the body darker still would drop it under 3:1 on the lighter
  indigo square, so the dark feel comes from the shade and the outline.
- **The glitch hid the piece.** The first burst drew its colour-split copies over
  the enemy. Now they sit behind it and only their offset edges show; only the
  sliced band and the noise go on top.
- **The hatch was the loudest thing on the board.** It is thinner and fainter
  now, but it still reads on both squares (1.59:1 at its weakest).
- **Neon fills vanished on dark squares.** The fill is now 40% of the tone, not
  24%.

Weaknesses still there, for step 1:

- On dark squares the bishop is the dimmest piece at 20px in every tile.
- The waypoint is a ring, like the goal's ring and the hidden-goal rings. Colour
  and a ✓ tell them apart, and in greyscale only the solid-against-dashed line
  does. The waypoint probably needs its own shape.
- The pit reads as a dark ring, which is clear on the dark square and weaker on
  the light one.
- In neon, the glow is invisible at 20px, so only the lit outline carries there.
- The hero and the enemies share a luminance band in the light theme (1.37:1).
  They are told apart by shape and a magenta-or-cyan hue as well, which is within
  the brief, but it's the tightest margin.

## The voice guide *(proposal)*

The flavour lives in the chrome; the teaching stays plain (`DESIGN.md` §9).

**Rules**

1. **Flavour is for chrome only.** Titles, tier and chapter headings, folder
   names, the start menu, the level-complete and promotion moments, theme names.
2. **Teaching text is never styled.** Lessons, hints, error translations, the
   Codex and tooltips keep today's wording. A clever line that slows
   understanding is a bug.
3. **A button says what it does.** Anything clickable keeps a plain label:
   "Run", "Next level →", "Reset progress".
4. **The player writes the program; enemies are corrupted code.** Never "you
   failed": a run is lost, an enemy or a pit did it.
5. **Short and flat.** A heading is a few words. No exclamation chains, no
   jargon that isn't Python's own.

**Before and after** (the "before" strings are in the game today)

| Where | Before | SNES-crisp | Neon vector | Terminal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | Learn Python by moving a chess piece | You write the program. The pawn runs it. | You are the program. Make the pawn move. | `$ python pawn.py   # you write pawn.py` |
| Solved headline | Solved! | Level cleared! | RUN COMPLETE | exit 0 · all checks passed |
| Lost headline | Lost | Run lost | RUN CORRUPTED | exit 1 · run failed |
| Tier heading | pawn | Pawn tier | TIER 01 — PAWN | pawn/  (6 chapters) |
| Chapter heading | Chapter 2 · Counting Steps | Chapter 2 · Counting Steps | CH.02 :: COUNTING STEPS | ch02_counting_steps/ |
| Lessons | the level list | Lessons | // LESSONS | $ ls lessons/ |
| Themes (Settings) | Dark, Light | Night shift, Daylight terminal | same | same |
| Coming soon | (new) | coming soon | soon | not yet built |

**Stays exactly as it is:** "Not there yet", "Check the rules", "Python
stopped", "Endless loop", "Next level →", every lesson and hint, every friendly
error, every Codex entry, every tooltip, and the label on every button. The
error card's headline is one of these: it is plain, and the corruption is only
a flicker on its frame.

## Proposal: the `#/styleguide` route

A page like `#/harness`, built in step 1 and kept as the place to check any new
tile, piece or animation. It would show:

- the colour tokens as swatches, in both themes, each with its contrast badge
  from the check below
- every tile, piece and skin at 64px and at the minimum size, on a dark and a
  light square, as in the style tiles' sprite sheets
- enemies with their corruption, with a button to hold the glitch frame
- a toggle for each state class (open, crossed, collected, bridged, gone, lost,
  celebrate, bumping, reading, refusing)
- a replay button for each motion-kit entry (step 2)
- greyscale, reduced-motion and CRT toggles
- the same sprite registry the level editor's palette will read, so M4 reuses it

It would not run Python or load a level. It is built from the registry, so
adding a tile in a later tier puts it on the page with no extra work.

## Proposal: the automated contrast check

`design/contrast.mjs` is the working draft. In step 4 it becomes
`scripts/check-contrast.mjs`, joins `npm run check`, and changes in three ways:

- it reads `src/styles.css`, the game's real tokens, not the prototype's
- its table of pairs (the checks above) is the contract, and a failure names the
  pair, the theme and the numbers
- it checks the final Settings choices as well: every theme name, and the CRT
  option's overlay

It parses `light-dark()`, `var()` and alpha colours with no dependencies, uses
the WCAG relative-luminance formula, and runs in well under a second.

## For the designer: what to look at and compare

1. **The three tiles, side by side, in each theme.** Open `design/index.html`
   and go through the board, then the sprite sheet. Do the pawn, knight and
   enemies feel like "armoured figure" and "corrupted data"?
2. **The greyscale and 20px strips.** Can you tell every piece and tile apart?
3. **The glitch**, with the held frame and without, and the reduced-motion view.
   Is it enough to draw the eye without tiring it?
4. **The start menu**: arrow keys and Enter. Is the arcade title what you meant?
5. **The Lessons directory**, the plain explorer (tiles 1 and 3) against the
   hybrid with the detail pane (tile 2). Open and close folders, click a row.
6. **The voice**, in the table above and on each tile.
7. **The proposals** for `#/styleguide` and the contrast check.

## Open questions

- **The logo's magenta shadow.** Magenta is hostile-only, but the brief asks for
  a magenta shadow on the logo. `DESIGN.md` records it as the one chrome
  exception, marked "to confirm".
- **The light theme's sprites.** To pass 3:1 on pale squares, the daylight
  sprites run darker than the dark theme's. Same shapes and hues, softer
  everywhere else, but the sprites are not the same tones.
- **Free Play and Level Editor on the menu.** In the mock the arrow keys land on
  them and Enter says "coming soon". Should they be skipped by the arrows instead?
- **The waypoint's shape**, as noted above.
- **The type.** Silkscreen is capitals only. Press Start 2P is the widest, and
  VT323 the most readable at small sizes. All three are OFL and self-hosted, so
  none needs a network request.
