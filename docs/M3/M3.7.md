## Step 0, round 6: the full style sheet *(Designer, 2026-10-02)*

Round 5 is approved as the style for the full set. Before anything reaches the
game, the designer asked for one full style sheet in it, as a dev page like the
earlier full tiles, in both themes: the title screen, the board with every
sprite and the threats, every sprite on a dark and a light square, the code
panel, the error messages, the success message, the Lessons menu and the chrome
headings. The halftone dots scale with the board. Page:
`/design/sheet.html`; screenshot `docs/M3/M3.7/shots/round6-style-sheet.png`.
Then stop for review; the game is not touched.

**Built.** The board is the real `BoardView` on the round 1 sample (every
Pawn-tier tile, a rook, a bishop, a chaser with its route, threatened squares),
dressed by `design/sheet/dress.ts`; every state button still works.
- **Sprites** (`design/sheet/models.ts`, `draw.ts`, `sprites.ts`): clean wire for
  the world (violet walls and pit, amber gates, plank, waypoint, gem, green sign
  and rune), round 5's solid hero with its brackets and the knight skin drawn
  the same way (a solid horse-head helm), and broken magenta wire for the rook,
  bishop and chaser, each glitching on its own beat.
- **The goal** is the board's one beacon: an amber wire flag on a lit pad with a
  column of light rising from it, its wire heavier and brighter than the rest.
- **The halftone scales with the board:** dots are sized in squares (six across a
  square); a board drawn under 32px a square switches to four bigger dots across,
  so the 20px board and the Lessons detail board keep visible dots. The first
  pass was too loud, so the dots were made smaller and fainter.
- **The title** keeps the decided composition (chrome logo, sunset, the enemy rank
  before the sun, the hero under the menu) in the new materials. The wire rank
  vanished against the sunset, so on the title each enemy is backlit: a dark
  shape behind its broken wire.
- **The cards** use the engine's own wording: a Python error with its traceback,
  a lost run, "Not there yet", and the success flourish above the plain card.

## Step 1: Foundations *(proposed; after the style sheet is approved)*

Step 1 builds the approved style into the game itself, on this branch, and
stops for review before step 2. Nothing merges until the whole milestone has
been checked.

**What it builds**
1. **Tokens** (`src/styles.css`): the noir palette and the colour roles for the
   dark theme, and a "daylight terminal" light theme (pale squares, the same
   wire shapes in deeper violet, magenta and amber, no glow; the solid hero in
   a deeper cyan with a dark edge instead of a glowing one). The contrast check
   (`scripts/check-contrast.mjs`, from `design/contrast.mjs`) joins
   `npm run check` now, so every token after this is checked.
2. **The board** (`src/ui/board.ts`): the lit grid (thin lines, crossing ticks,
   a faint glow on the outer edge), restyled file and rank labels, and the
   halftone on threatened squares.
   - **The halftone scales with the board:** the dots are sized in squares, not
     pixels (about six across a square), and a board drawn under about 32px a
     square switches to a coarser grid with bigger dots, so a dot never shrinks
     below about 1.5px. The 20px greyscale crop checks it.
3. **A sprite system** (`src/ui/sprites/`): the small 3D mesh kit from
   `design/directions/mesh.ts`, three ways of drawing it (wire for the world,
   solid for the hero, broken wire for enemies), and a registry from each tile
   kind, enemy kind and skin to its drawing. Colours come from tokens only.
4. **Every Pawn-tier sprite in the style:**
   - wall (round 4's clean violet wire), sign and rune (green wire: a screen on
     a post, a tablet with `>_`)
   - guarded and timed gates, plank, gem (amber wire)
   - pit (a violet wire funnel falling away)
   - waypoint (an amber diamond, crossed in green)
   - **the goal, made eye-catching (my proposal):** the board's one beacon,
     an amber wire flag on a lit amber pad with a thin column of light rising
     from it. The column makes it findable across a 12×12 board. Its slow pulse
     arrives with step 2's motion; with reduced motion it stays still and bright.
   - hidden-goal spots: amber dashed rings with `?`, then ✓ or ✗
   - enemies as broken magenta wire: rook, bishop, patrol and chaser, each
     damaged at rest, with round 4's occasional break-and-snap
   - the hero: round 5's solid pawn with its glowing outline and floor shadow,
     and the **knight skin** drawn the same way (a solid horse-head helm),
     registered as a skin (§8)
5. **Facing through the brackets:** round 5's HUD brackets replace the
   rotating pointer, and the model turns to face that way.
6. **Everything kept:** every state class (open, crossed, collected, bridged,
   gone, lost, celebrate, bumping, reading, refusing), the badges and the
   tooltips, restyled. Small boards (lesson snippets, side boards) get the same
   sprites with lighter detail.
7. **`#/styleguide`**, the review page: every tile, piece, enemy and skin at full
   size and at 20px, in both themes, with toggles for each state, the enemy's
   break frames, and a greyscale switch.

**Left for later steps:** step 2's motion (moves, the trail, the rook's capture,
the goal's pulse, gates and runes animating); step 3's chrome (the start menu,
the Lessons directory, cards, the promotion ceremony, Settings → Piece and the
skin unlock); step 4's polish.

**Checks:** vitest for the registry (every tile kind and enemy kind has a
drawing) and for facing; `npm run check` with the contrast check; the existing
level tests are unaffected (no engine changes). The e2e checks are written in
the pre-merge pass, as usual.

**What was built:** tile 4, "Neon noir", at

### What the readability check found

The first drafts failed in ways worth recording:

- **Light theme sprites were too pale.** The hero was 1.13:1 against the dark
  square. In the light theme the sprites' tones now run darker, so a body reads
  against both squares.
- **Enemies were bright pink, not "magenta on a dark body".** The body is now a
  mid magenta with a bright left rim and a dark shade, and cracks and dead pixels
  on top. Making the body darker still would drop it under 3:1 on the lighter
  indigo square, so the dark feel comes from the shade and the outline.
- **The glitch hid the piece.** The first burst drew its colour-split copies over
  the enemy. Now they sit behind it and only their offset edges show; only the
  sliced band and the noise go on top.
- **The hatch was the loudest thing on the board.** It is thinner and fainter
  now, but it still reads on both squares (1.59:1 at its weakest).
- **Neon fills vanished on dark squares.** The fill is now 40% of the tone, not
  24%.

Weaknesses still there, for step 1:

- On dark squares the bishop is the dimmest piece at 20px in every tile.
- The waypoint is a ring, like the goal's ring and the hidden-goal rings. Colour
  and a ✓ tell them apart, and in greyscale only the solid-against-dashed line
  does. The waypoint probably needs its own shape.
- The pit reads as a dark ring, which is clear on the dark square and weaker on
  the light one.
- In neon, the glow is invisible at 20px, so only the lit outline carries there.
- The hero and the enemies share a luminance band in the light theme (1.37:1).
  They are told apart by shape and a magenta-or-cyan hue as well, which is within
  the brief, but it's the tightest margin.

## The voice guide *(proposal)*

The flavour lives in the chrome; the teaching stays plain (`DESIGN.md` §9).

**Rules**

1. **Flavour is for chrome only.** Titles, tier and chapter headings, folder
   names, the start menu, the level-complete and promotion moments, theme names.
2. **Teaching text is never styled.** Lessons, hints, error translations, the
   Codex and tooltips keep today's wording. A clever line that slows
   understanding is a bug.
3. **A button says what it does.** Anything clickable keeps a plain label:
   "Run", "Next level →", "Reset progress".
4. **The player writes the program; enemies are corrupted code.** Never "you
   failed": a run is lost, an enemy or a pit did it.
5. **Short and flat.** A heading is a few words. No exclamation chains, no
   jargon that isn't Python's own.

**Before and after** (the "before" strings are in the game today)

| Where | Before | SNES-crisp | Neon vector | Terminal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | Learn Python by moving a chess piece | You write the program. The pawn runs it. | You are the program. Make the pawn move. | `$ python pawn.py   # you write pawn.py` |
| Solved headline | Solved! | Level cleared! | RUN COMPLETE | exit 0 · all checks passed |
| Lost headline | Lost | Run lost | RUN CORRUPTED | exit 1 · run failed |
| Tier heading | pawn | Pawn tier | TIER 01 — PAWN | pawn/  (6 chapters) |
| Chapter heading | Chapter 2 · Counting Steps | Chapter 2 · Counting Steps | CH.02 :: COUNTING STEPS | ch02_counting_steps/ |
| Lessons | the level list | Lessons | // LESSONS | $ ls lessons/ |
| Themes (Settings) | Dark, Light | Night shift, Daylight terminal | same | same |
| Coming soon | (new) | coming soon | soon | not yet built |

**Stays exactly as it is:** "Not there yet", "Check the rules", "Python
stopped", "Endless loop", "Next level →", every lesson and hint, every friendly
error, every Codex entry, every tooltip, and the label on every button. The
error card's headline is one of these: it is plain, and the corruption is only
a flicker on its frame.

## Proposal: the `#/styleguide` route

A page like `#/harness`, built in step 1 and kept as the place to check any new
tile, piece or animation. It would show:

- the colour tokens as swatches, in both themes, each with its contrast badge
  from the check below
- every tile, piece and skin at 64px and at the minimum size, on a dark and a
  light square, as in the style tiles' sprite sheets
- enemies with their corruption, with a button to hold the glitch frame
- a toggle for each state class (open, crossed, collected, bridged, gone, lost,
  celebrate, bumping, reading, refusing)
- a replay button for each motion-kit entry (step 2)
- greyscale, reduced-motion and CRT toggles
- the same sprite registry the level editor's palette will read, so M4 reuses it

It would not run Python or load a level. It is built from the registry, so
adding a tile in a later tier puts it on the page with no extra work.

## Proposal: the automated contrast check

`design/contrast.mjs` is the working draft. In step 4 it becomes
`scripts/check-contrast.mjs`, joins `npm run check`, and changes in three ways:

- it reads `src/styles.css`, the game's real tokens, not the prototype's
- its table of pairs (the checks above) is the contract, and a failure names the
  pair, the theme and the numbers
- it checks the final Settings choices as well: every theme name, and the CRT
  option's overlay

It parses `light-dark()`, `var()` and alpha colours with no dependencies, uses
the WCAG relative-luminance formula, and runs in well under a second.

## For the designer: what to look at and compare

1. **The three tiles, side by side, in each theme.** Open `design/index.html`
   and go through the board, then the sprite sheet. Do the pawn, knight and
   enemies feel like "armoured figure" and "corrupted data"?
2. **The greyscale and 20px strips.** Can you tell every piece and tile apart?
3. **The glitch**, with the held frame and without, and the reduced-motion view.
   Is it enough to draw the eye without tiring it?
4. **The start menu**: arrow keys and Enter. Is the arcade title what you meant?
5. **The Lessons directory**, the plain explorer (tiles 1 and 3) against the
   hybrid with the detail pane (tile 2). Open and close folders, click a row.
6. **The voice**, in the table above and on each tile.
7. **The proposals** for `#/styleguide` and the contrast check.

## Open questions

- **The logo's magenta shadow.** Magenta is hostile-only, but the brief asks for
  a magenta shadow on the logo. `DESIGN.md` records it as the one chrome
  exception, marked "to confirm".
- **The light theme's sprites.** To pass 3:1 on pale squares, the daylight
  sprites run darker than the dark theme's. Same shapes and hues, softer
  everywhere else, but the sprites are not the same tones.
- **Free Play and Level Editor on the menu.** In the mock the arrow keys land on
  them and Enter says "coming soon". Should they be skipped by the arrows instead?
- **The waypoint's shape**, as noted above.
- **The type.** Silkscreen is capitals only. Press Start 2P is the widest, and
  VT323 the most readable at small sizes. All three are OFL and self-hosted, so
  none needs a network request.

the Piece setting. The prototype's `design/` folder goes once its content has
moved.
