# M3.5 · Chapter 5, Keep Going: level specs

Branch `m3-5-chapter-5`. This is **checkpoint 1**: the designer approves these
specs before the levels are built. They build on `docs/M3/M3.md` → Chapter 5.

Decided *(Designer, 2026-10-02)*, before the specs:
- **`break` is forced by par.** Findings below show par can't do that on its
  own (open question 1).
- **`continue` is taught and accepted, not forced.**
- **No ice.** Level 5.2 uses what's already built.

The specs give each level's concept, boards, rules, par and what makes it
necessary. They never give solution code. References, wrong attempts, hints
and solution notes are written blind when the levels are built. Every board
below was checked with the real engine, with counts only printed:
- each board can be solved on its own (by plain calls, or by a program that
  uses the loops the level teaches);
- no board's plain-call solution works on any other board, so the code has to
  decide as it goes;
- the **shortest program in a small search** (`scripts/gen_while.py probe`) is
  given for each level, along with the shortest that avoids `while`.

Par is provisional until the reference solution is written.

## Already built, before the specs

- **Friendly errors for the mistakes `while` brings** (`engine/rankfile/errors.py`,
  tests in `engine/tests/test_errors.py`):
  - `break` or `continue` outside a loop: "only works inside a loop".
  - An endless `while`: the "never finished" message now says a `while`
    repeats as long as its condition is True. When the condition uses an
    ability without its parentheses (`while pawn.at_goal:`, which is never
    False), it says so and names the fix. A `for` loop gets no `while` hint.
  - A `while not pawn.at_goal:` with no parentheses runs zero times instead
    (`not` of a method is False), so the run reads "incomplete". That case
    isn't translated yet (open question 4).
- **`scripts/gen_while.py`**, the search core. It enumerates small programs
  (`while`, `for` over a fixed `range()`, `if`/`else`, `break`, `continue`)
  on the real engine and prints only counts. The reference writer comes
  after approval, as in M3.4.

## What Chapter 5 teaches, and what makes each part necessary

| Level | Trains | What makes it necessary |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 Until You Arrive | `while`, with `not pawn.at_goal()` | the goal is hidden on one of three squares, so the pawn has to stop when it gets there |
| 5.2 The Winding Road | `while`, when the rounds aren't known ahead | four roads with 2, 3, 4 and 3 legs, so no fixed number of rounds works |
| 5.3 Counting Steps, Again | a counter changed inside a `while` | a guard asks how far you walked, which differs on every board and which no ability reports |
| 5.4 Which Rook? | `break`, and `continue` shown | the goal is hidden under one of the rooks; see open question 1 |
| 5.5 The Staircase | `while` on `look("right")` | a line of rooks whose length differs on every board, including none |
| 5.6 The Tally *(mastery, optional)* | everything above | the staircase, a counter, and a guard who asks for the count |

Findings that shape the chapter:

- **A `for` loop can stand in for a `while`** if its body guards itself with an
  `if`. That costs a line, so **a line limit equal to par** keeps `while` the
  only way through on 5.1, 5.2 and 5.5. 5.3 and 5.6 are shaped by their boards
  instead.
- **`squares_ahead()` doesn't stop at pits, enemies or the goal.** 5.1 and 5.2
  rely on that: it measures the hall, not where the goal is.
- **A chaser doesn't suit `while`.** The pawn only sees the squares next to it,
  and whether a chaser lands diagonal (capturable) or beside the pawn depends
  on timing. A reacting loop that solves several boards didn't exist among the
  shapes I searched. The outline's chaser level is replaced by the staircase
  of rooks (open question 2).
- **`break` can't be forced by par.** See open question 1.

## Proposed changes to the outline (for approval)

1. **5.2 is a winding road, not "ice or conditions".** No ice, as decided.
   The road bends left a different number of times and by different amounts
   on each board.
2. **5.5 is the staircase of rooks, not a chaser** (see above).
3. **5.4 is "Which Rook?"** and the mastery is "The Tally", which joins
   5.3's counter to 5.5's staircase. Both depend on the answers to open
   questions 1 and 2.

## The levels

In every map `P` is the start and `G` the goal, `#` is a wall and `X` a guard's
gate. The pawn starts facing north.

### 5.1 · Until You Arrive

- **Trains:** `while`: repeat while a condition is True. With
  `not pawn.at_goal()`, which was taught in 4.1.
- **Brief:** "The goal is hidden. It might be on any of the three squares
  marked ?."
- **Board** (3×10, one board with three cases: the goal on each `?`):
  ```
  # ? #
  # . #
  # . #
  # ? #
  # . #
  # . #
  # . #
  # ? #
  # . #
  # P #
  ```
- **Abilities:** `move`, `turn_left`, `turn_right`, `wait`, `at_goal`.
- **Rules:** at most 2 lines of code (the limit equals par).
- **Par:** 2. In the search, the shortest program is 2 lines and uses
  `while`; without `while` it's 3.
- **Must fail:**
  - a fixed number of steps (stops short on one case and passes the goal
    on another)
  - walking until the wall (the same, overshoots)
- **Lesson** (on a lesson board with a goal hidden on two squares):
  - `while`, its condition and its indented lines, with `print()` showing
    the condition change.
  - A `run error` snippet: a loop whose condition never goes False
    ("never finished"), which is how every `while` mistake looks.

### 5.2 · The Winding Road

- **Trains:** `while` when the number of rounds isn't known ahead: here, how
  many legs the road has. Each leg is crossed with `squares_ahead()`.
- **Brief:** "The road bends left, and on every board it bends a different
  number of times. The goal is at the end."
- **Boards** (four roads that bend left 1, 2, 3 and 2 times, over different
  lengths; a leg always ends at a wall):
  ```
  board 1      board 2        board 3        board 4
  # # # # # #  # # # # # # #  # # # # # # #  # # # # #
  # G . . . #  # . . . . . #  # . . . . . #  # . . . #
  # # # # . #  # . # # # . #  # . # # # . #  # . # . #
  # # # # . #  # G # # # . #  # . # # # . #  # . # . #
  # # # # . #  # # # # # P #  # . . G # . #  # . # . #
  # # # # . #  # # # # # # #  # # # # # . #  # . # P #
  # # # # . #                 # # # # # P #  # G # # #
  # # # # P #                 # # # # # # #  # # # # #
  # # # # # #
  ```
  The number of legs differs on every board, so a fixed number of rounds
  (one leg per round, with `squares_ahead()`) fails. Board 3 spirals in,
  so that its last leg ends at a wall. (The maps are drawn by
  `scripts/gen_while.py`, so the exact lengths may be tuned while building.)
- **Abilities:** as 5.1, plus `squares_ahead` (taught in Chapter 2) and `look`.
- **Rules:** at most 3 lines (the limit equals par).
- **Par:** 3. In the search, the shortest program is 3 lines and uses
  `while`; without `while` it's 4 (a `for` with a guard inside).
- **Must fail:**
  - one board's moves typed out (the boards differ)
  - a fixed number of rounds (stops short on the longer roads, and walks
    on past the goal on the shorter ones)
- **Lesson:** a `while` on a lesson board with a bend, using
  `squares_ahead()` to cross each leg, and a `run lost` snippet that
  repeats a fixed number of rounds.

### 5.3 · Counting Steps, Again

- **Trains:** a counter: a variable changed on every pass of a `while`.
- **Brief:** "A guard stands on the road. He wants to know how far you
  walked to get here, and the road is different on every board."
- **Boards** (four roads, the guard on the square before the goal; he asks
  "How many squares did you walk to get here?", and the answer differs on
  every board):
  ```
  board 1      board 2        board 3        board 4
  # # # # # #  # # # # # # #  # # # # # # #  # # # # #
  # G X . . #  # . . . . . #  # . . . . . #  # . . . #
  # # # # . #  # X # # # . #  # . # # # . #  # . # . #
  # # # # . #  # G # # # . #  # . # # # . #  # . # . #
  # # # # . #  # # # # # P #  # . X G # . #  # . # . #
  # # # # . #  # # # # # # #  # # # # # . #  # X # P #
  # # # # . #                 # # # # # P #  # G # # #
  # # # # P #                 # # # # # # #  # # # # #
  # # # # # #
  ```
- **Abilities:** as 5.2.
- **Rules:** none.
- **Par:** 9. The search found one 9-line program (the counter's setup, a
  loop with two branches, the answer and the last steps) and nothing
  shorter, including counting with `squares_ahead()`.
- **Must fail:**
  - board 1's answer typed in (wrong on the others)
  - counting only the first leg (a miscount; the guard says so)
- **Lesson:** a counter beside a `while` on a lesson board, with the count
  printed, and a `run error` snippet that uses the counter before it's set.

### 5.4 · Which Rook?

- **Trains:** leaving a loop early with `break`. `continue` skips the rest
  of one pass; the lesson shows both.
- **Brief:** "A staircase of rooks, and the goal is hidden under one of
  them. Take rooks until you find it."
- **Board** (7×8; four cases, the goal on each of the first four rooks;
  rooks on b2 to f6):
  ```
  . . . . . . .
  . . . . . R .      R: a rook (listed separately)
  . . . . R . .      ?: the goal might be under any of the first four
  . . . ? . . .
  . . ? . . . .
  . ? . . . . .
  . . . . . . .
  P . . . . . .
  ```
- **Abilities:** `move`, `turn_left`, `turn_right`, `wait`, `at_goal`,
  `look`, `capture_left`, `capture_right`.
- **Rules:** see open question 1.
- **Par:** 2 with a plain `while`, 4 with `break`. In the search, the
  4-line programs that use `break` (51 of them) all solve it.
- **Must fail:** taking a fixed number of rooks, and capturing past the goal
  (a rook beyond it attacks the square).
- **Lesson:** a `while True:` loop that stops itself with `break`, and
  `continue` skipping a pass, on a lesson board with a few rooks.

### 5.5 · The Staircase

- **Trains:** `while pawn.look("right") == "rook":`, with `capture_right()`.
  A loop that runs as many times as the board says, including none.
- **Brief:** "A line of rooks climbs the board, one step up and one step
  across. Take them all. The goal is straight above the last one."
- **Boards** (7×8; 2, 0, 3 and 5 rooks; the pawn on a1):
  ```
  2 rooks      0 rooks      3 rooks      5 rooks
  . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . G .
  . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . G . . .   . . . . . . .
  . . G . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . .   G . . . . . .   . . . . . . .   . . . . . . .
  P . . . . . .   P . . . . . .   P . . . . . .   P . . . . . .
  ```
  The rooks stand on b2, c3 and so on up the diagonal, one fewer than the
  goal's row.
- **Abilities:** as 5.4.
- **Rules:** at most 3 lines (the limit equals par).
- **Par:** 3. In the search, 3 lines with `while`; without it, 4.
- **Must fail:** a fixed number of captures (a `CaptureError` on the short
  boards, rooks left on the long ones), and walking on without taking them
  (taken by the rook).
- **Lesson:** `look("right")` and a `while` on it, on a lesson board with a
  short staircase, and a `run lost` snippet that walks straight on.

### 5.6 · The Tally *(mastery, optional)*

- **Trains:** 5.3's counter, 5.5's loop on `look()`, and the last steps.
- **Brief:** "Take every rook on the staircase, then tell the guard how
  many you took."
- **Boards** (the staircase of 5.5, with a guard on the square straight above
  the last rook and the goal above him; he asks "How many rooks did you
  take?"; 2, 1, 3 and 5 rooks):
  ```
  2 rooks
  . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . .
  . . G . . . .
  . . X . . . .
  . . . . . . .      the rooks stand on b2 and c3
  . . . . . . .
  P . . . . . .
  ```
- **Abilities:** as 5.4.
- **Rules:** at most 8 lines.
- **Par:** 6. In the search, the loop body is 3 lines and needs `while`;
  with the counter's setup and the guard's answer it's 6.
- **Must fail:** the first board's answer typed in, and counting by the
  board's rooks (wrong when a rook is missing).

## Open questions

1. **How to make `break` necessary.** Par can't: on 5.4, a plain
   `while not pawn.at_goal():` is 2 lines and the `break` form is 4. Rooks,
   pits and guards all behave the same way, because a `while` loop's own
   condition does the same job whenever the exit test can be asked before
   the loop body.
   - **Recommended:** 5.4 as drawn, with the rule "Must use `break`" (the
     same kind of rule as 2.4's f-strings), and the lesson says when it's
     the clearer way. `continue` stays lesson-only.
   - Alternative: no rule. 5.4 teaches `break` in its lesson and hints, but
     a player who solves it in 2 lines is right to.
   - Alternative: find a board where `break` is shorter. The condition is a
     test that can only be asked partway through a pass, after several lines
     of work that can't be repeated before it. I haven't found a natural
     one.
2. **The chaser.** The outline had a chaser in 5.5 and the mastery.
   - **Recommended:** drop it from Chapter 5. It would need a way to see it
     coming (a longer-range `look()`), and it fits a later tier better.
   - Alternative: a chaser on a corridor of pits, as pressure only: it
     forbids waiting, but nothing in the code reacts to it.
3. **Line limits equal to par on 5.1, 5.2 and 5.5.** That's what keeps
   `while` the only way through, since a `for` loop with an `if` is one line
   longer. Alternative: no limit, and the stars decide.
4. **`while not pawn.at_goal:` with no parentheses runs zero times.** The
   run reads "incomplete", with no hint. **Recommended:** add one, as an
   `ast` check, naming the missing `()`.
5. **Titles and briefs,** as above, or changed.
