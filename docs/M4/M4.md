# M4 · Sound, the level editor, free play and the Pawn tier's mastery set: plan and decisions

M4 follows the Pawn tier (M3, complete) and the first design pass (M3.7). It
was planned as "the level editor and the Pawn tier's mastery set" (`DESIGN.md`
§7). On 2026-10-07 the designer brought sound into it and asked for levels
that can be shared. This document is the plan for the whole milestone. Each
sub-milestone gets its own spec (`docs/M4/M4.<n>.md`) before it's built.

*(Designer, date)* marks a decision. *(Recommended)* marks a recommendation
that waits for the designer's answer in a sub-milestone's spec. What is still
open is at the end.

## How M4 runs

As M3 did:

- **One branch, PR, play-test and QA round per sub-milestone.** Merged
  branches are kept (see M4.0).
- **A spec first.** Each sub-milestone's spec is approved before it's built.
  M4.0 is small enough to build straight from this document.
- **Design choices are shown first.** Sounds, and the editor's and free
  play's layouts, are tried on dev pages in `design/`, shown to the designer,
  and go into the game only when the designer says so (the M3.7 rule).
- **The pre-merge pass** (`/simplify`, e2e checks for the user-visible
  changes, the full `npm run e2e`) runs once per PR, when the designer calls
  the sub-milestone feature-complete. Then a retest list, then the merge.
  M4.0 has no pass of its own: M4.1's pass covers M4.0 too *(Designer,
  2026-10-07)*.
- **No spoilers.** The editor and the solver never show a curriculum level's
  hints, references or notes. Challenge levels are written the way chapters
  were: the designer approves specs, and references, naive attempts, hints
  and notes are written blind.

## M4.0 Startup

### Branches stay

Every branch is fully merged into `main` (checked 2026-10-07): 2 local and 17
on GitHub. The first draft deleted them, but the designer would rather keep
them unless there's a reason not to *(Designer, 2026-10-07)*. There isn't one:
- They cost nothing.
- They don't affect GitHub Pages, which deploys from `main` through
  `.github/workflows/pages.yml`, not from a branch.
- Every PR page links to its branch.

The only cost is a longer branch list. M4's branches follow the same rule.

### The "waits rather than spinning" test, on every level

`test_reference_solution_waits_rather_than_spinning` in
`engine/tests/test_levels.py` used to skip a level whose piece can't
`wait()`. It now runs on every level *(Designer, 2026-10-07)*:
- A reference never turns more than it needs to between one step and the next.
- It doesn't turn after its last step either, with one exception: a loop's
  last lap may end on the loop's own turn, because that turn comes from a line
  that already ran. A turn from a line running for the first time (one added
  at the end to pass time) still fails.
- All 45 levels pass. Without that exception, one Testing-ground level would
  fail: its reference is a single loop whose lap ends on a turn, and the
  level's only clock counts new lines, so that last turn neither passes time
  nor moves anything.
- Made-up programs (not level solutions) check the test itself: spinning
  between steps fails, an added turn at the end fails, waiting passes, and a
  loop ending on its turn passes.

This isn't something a player sees. Only the level checker changed, and no
level or solution did.

### QA-042: "Show me a solution" after three tries

**The rule.** "Show me a solution…" is offered once **three runs of the level
have failed**, whether or not any hint is open. It stays on offer until the
level is solved or a solution has been seen.
- A failed run is any run that doesn't solve the level, as today: incomplete,
  lost, an error, out of lines, or a broken rule.
- Replaying a recording doesn't count, as today. Help records each run once,
  from `run()` in `levelView.ts`.
- Unchanged:
  - Hints open one at a time, and the first still gives up the third star.
  - The confirm step stays.
  - Seeing a solution still marks the level solved with help, with no stars
    until the player solves it themselves.

**How:**
- `src/progress.ts`:
  - `failedAfterHints` becomes `failedRuns`, counted from the first run.
  - `sanitize` carries an old saved `failedAfterHints` over into `failedRuns`,
    so nobody loses an offer they already had.
  - The rule lives in the store, where vitest can check it:
    `offersSolution(id)` and `recordFailedRun(id)`, with
    `GIVE_UP_AFTER = 3`.
- `src/ui/help.ts`:
  - The hints no longer gate the offer.
  - The offer has its own place in the panel's Solution section, so a level
    with no hints gets it too. It used to live inside the hints section,
    which is empty on a level without hints.
  - After the first failed run, a quiet line there counts down: "Still stuck
    after 2 more runs? Then you can ask to see a solution."
- The outcome card of a failed run offers "Need a hint?" while hints remain,
  and "Show me a solution…" once it's on offer. Both can show at once, and
  each focuses its own button in the panel (`outcomeActions`, `focus(on)`).
- Tests:
  - vitest (`progress.test.ts`):
    - the offer after three failed runs with no hint open
    - no more counting once it's made
    - none once the level is solved or a solution seen
    - the carry-over from `failedAfterHints`
  - e2e (`scripts/e2e/app.check.mjs`): the M2 giving-up checks asserted the
    old rule, so they change with it (as QA-033's did). They no longer open
    the hints first, and they look for the offer in the Solution section.
    "QA-042: the third failed run offers 'Show me a solution', with no hint
    open" replaces the old check. The app suite ran them (142/142). M4.0's
    retest is enough to merge it; the full e2e run waits for M4.1's
    pre-merge pass.
- Docs: `DESIGN.md` §2 rule 5, and the header comment in `help.ts`.

**Retest list:** in `docs/QA.md`, QA-042.

## M4.1 Sound

**Decided** *(Designer, 2026-10-07)*:
- the track and its credit
- effects in M4
- the defaults: on, quiet, starting at the first click or key, a top-bar mute,
  a volume each

### The music and its licence

White Bat Audio's FAQ, licence agreement and Karl Casey's Bandcamp page were
read on 2026-10-07:
- **Allowed:** free use in creative projects, credited as "Music by Karl Casey
  @ White Bat Audio".
- **Not allowed:**
  - Selling the soundtrack, or distributing it apart from the game (for
    example on Spotify or other streaming services).
  - Re-releasing, sampling or remixing it as music.
  - AI training.
- **"Major" film and game projects** need to contact him for a sync licence.
  A free, non-commercial learning game isn't one, but if the game ever goes
  commercial, ask him first.
- **Where the file comes from:** his Bandcamp, where his releases are free
  downloads, not a recording of the YouTube video. The designer downloads
  "Elysium" and hands it over.
- **How it ships:**
  - Re-encoded small (about 128 kbps, a few MB) into `public/audio/`.
  - Fetched at runtime through `import.meta.env.BASE_URL`, and only when
    music is on. Never in the bundle.
  - The repo is public, so the file can be downloaded from it. That is
    distribution with the game, but the credit file must say it isn't free
    to reuse apart from it.

The credit goes in four places:
- the start menu, as a small footer line
- Settings → Sound, next to the music's volume
- the README's License section
- `public/audio/CREDITS.md`, which also says the audio is not under the MIT
  licence or CC BY-NC-SA, is used under White Bat Audio's terms, and may not
  be reused apart from the game

### The music: a recommendation *(Recommended, 2026-10-07; confirm in M4.1's spec)*

The designer asked for a recommendation: "Elysium" is the one track for now,
and more are likely.

- **Now: one track, everywhere, never restarting.**
  - "Elysium" loops on the start menu, in Lessons and on levels.
  - The app shell owns the music, not a screen, so moving between screens
    never restarts it. A screen change only keeps it going.
  - The loop point gets a short fade, so the restart doesn't jar.
- **Built as a playlist from day one.**
  - The tracks are a small list in one place: file, title, credit.
  - With one track it just loops. Adding a track is one file in
    `public/audio/`, one line in the list, and its line in `CREDITS.md`.
- **When more tracks come:**
  - **A title theme:** the start menu gets its own track.
  - **A rotation for play:** Lessons, levels and free play play the rest one
    after another, shuffled, with a short crossfade, never the same track
    twice in a row.
  - **No per-chapter themes.** Six chapters and a handful of tracks would
    repeat anyway. A level can last a long time, and a change of track
    shouldn't be tied to switching levels.
  - **Even loudness:** each track is evened out when it's re-encoded, so
    switching never jumps in volume.
- **Settings → Sound shows what's playing:** "Now playing: Elysium · Music by
  Karl Casey @ White Bat Audio". A "Next track" button comes once there's more
  than one.
- **The big moments are effects, not music.** The promotion and Run complete
  get short stings from the sound effects. The music keeps playing under them,
  turned down for a moment.

### What gets built

- **One audio module** (`src/sound.ts`, or a `src/sound/` folder):
  - **Music:**
    - Plays through an `<audio>` element, so it streams instead of decoding
      the whole file.
    - Loops, fades in and out, and pauses while the tab is hidden.
  - **Effects:** synthesised with Web Audio (oscillators, noise, envelopes):
    chiptune that matches the look, with no sound files and nothing to
    license.
  - **Sound only starts after a click or key press:** browsers block it
    before that. The module waits for the first one.
- **Settings:**
  - `music` and `effects`, each a 0 to 100 slider (60 and 50 to start, set by the designer after listening; the
    designer chose sliders over steps, 2026-10-07).
  - A **mute button** in the top bar (a speaker icon in `icons.ts`,
    `aria-pressed`) silences both and remembers it.
- **Which events make a sound:**
  - Board events, the kinds in `protocol.ts`: `move`, `turn`, `bump`,
    `gate_open`, `guard`, `pick_up`, `bridge`, `capture`, `read`, and `lost`,
    `fall` and `crush`.
  - Outcomes: Run complete, each star, an error.
  - The chrome: moving and choosing in the start menu, opening a hint, a
    chapter's crown, the promotion.
- **When:**
  - Effects play only when playback animates an event forward, the same rule
    as the motion kit. Scrubbing, stepping back and jumping to the end are
    silent.
  - At high speeds, effects are thinned out so they don't pile up.
- **Accessibility:** sound never carries meaning alone; everything it marks
  is already on screen. Animations and sound are separate settings.

### Steps

0. **A sound sheet:** a dev page in `design/` with two or three takes on each
   effect, and the music's volume levels. The designer listens and picks.
1. The audio module, the settings, the mute button, the credit, the music.
2. The effects from the sound sheet, wired to playback and the chrome.
3. **The pre-merge pass, for M4.0 and M4.1 together** *(Designer,
   2026-10-07)*, once the designer calls M4.1 feature-complete:
   - `/simplify` over both milestones' code. If PR #18 (M4.0) has merged by
     then, M4.1's branch diff won't include M4.0's commits, so review from
     `6a10d66` (the last commit before M4.0) instead.
   - e2e checks for M4.1's user-visible changes.
   - The full `npm run e2e`.
   - A retest list covering both.

**Tests:**
- vitest: the settings and their defaults, and the event → sound table. The
  synthesiser is behind a small interface, so tests run without audio.
- e2e:
  - Chrome is started with `--mute-audio` (`scripts/e2e/cdp.mjs`).
  - Checks: the mute button, the Sound settings, and that the music file is
    fetched only when music is on.

## M4.2 and M4.3 The level editor

*(Designer, 2026-10-07)*: the editor is for players and the designer, and its
levels can be exported and shared.

### Principles

- **The engine is the only judge.** Every edit is checked by the real level
  checker (`levels.parse_level`, through the existing `loadLevel` request), so
  the editor never re-implements a rule. Its errors are shown where they
  belong: on the square, the enemy or the field.
- **One format.** The editor reads and writes the same YAML as `levels/`
  (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`). Nothing is editor-only.
- **The palette is the sprite registry.** `TILE_SPRITE` and `ENEMY_SPRITE` in
  `src/ui/sprites/index.ts` are the palette, plus the start, the goal and `?`
  squares. A new tile or enemy reaches the editor by being added there.
- **Real parts:**
  - The board is the real `BoardView`.
  - Test-play opens the real level screen: `mountLevel` with a `LevelSource`
    built from the draft.
- **Keyboard first,** in both themes, and fine with reduced motion.

### M4.2, the board

- **Where it lives:**
  - A route, `#/editor`, with **My levels**: drafts saved in the browser under
    their own key, apart from curriculum progress.
  - The start menu's Level Editor entry switches on.
- **What you can set:**
  - **The board:** a size up to 12×12; tiles painted from the palette; the
    start and its facing; the goal or `?` squares.
  - **Enemies:** kind, start, a route made by clicking its corners, loop,
    clock, armoured.
  - **Text:** what each rune, sign and gate says (a guard's question and
    answer, or a passphrase).
  - **The rest:** planks to start with; the title, the brief and the line
    saying what it trains; which abilities the piece has.
- **Undo and redo.**
- **Test-play,** in the real level screen.
- **Export and import** a `.yaml` file.
- **Copy a lesson level** as a starting point. It copies the board and the
  rules, **never the hints, the reference or the notes** (no spoilers).

### M4.3, the rules, a solvability check, and sharing

- **The rules:**
  - objectives
  - constraints (`max_lines`, required and banned constructs, `max_numbers`)
  - par and hints
  - other boards (variants, each with its own text and enemies)
- **A solvability check:**
  - `scripts/solve.py`'s search moves into the engine (`rankfile/search.py`).
    Then it can run in the worker, and the scripts import it from there.
  - It reports counts only: "solvable in 6 lines", or "no route found". It
    never shows the route or the code.
  - The worker's 3 s watchdog would stop a long search, so this request gets
    a cap on the states it explores and a longer time limit of its own.
  - As today, it can't judge code clocks (`line`, `new_line`), and says so.
- **Sharing, without a server** (accounts are out of scope, `DESIGN.md` §7):
  - **A share link,** `#/play/<code>`:
    - The level is compressed with the browser's built-in `CompressionStream`
      and written in base64url, so no new dependency is needed.
    - Opening the link plays the level in the real level screen, with "Open
      in the editor" to remix it.
    - It works on GitHub Pages. The biggest level in the game today, boards,
      text and hints included, is under 3 KB of YAML before compression.
  - **A share code:** the same text, for pasting into Import, where a long
    link might get cut off.
  - **A file:** the `.yaml` export.
  - **The author proves the level can be solved.** Before sharing, the author
    solves it in test-play. Their solving code travels with it as the level's
    solution, offered after three failed runs like any other (QA-042)
    *(Designer, 2026-10-07)*.
  - **Safety:**
    - Everything that arrives by link, code or file goes through the level
      checker, and has a size cap.
    - Its text is shown as plain text (`h()` text nodes), never as HTML or
      Markdown. A shared level has no Markdown lesson.
- **The designer's path into the repo:**
  - Under `npm run dev` only, "Save to the repo" writes
    `levels/<folder>/<id>.yaml` through a small Vite dev-server plugin. The
    production build doesn't contain it.
  - Curriculum and challenge references still come from the blind generators.
- **Progress** on editor and shared levels is kept apart from the
  curriculum's, and never earns a crown or a promotion. A shared level's
  progress is keyed by its content, so opening the same link twice finds it.
- **Later, if a server ever comes:** a public gallery, or "send this level to
  the designer".

## M4.4 Free play

### Why the mastery set lives in free play, not Lessons

The designer asked where the Pawn tier's mastery set belongs, whether it has
a lesson part, and what else free play holds. The answer below was accepted
*(Designer, 2026-10-07)*:

- **Lessons is the teaching path.** Every chapter brings in something new, and
  the tier ends at its promotion. A tier's mastery set teaches nothing new: it
  mixes what you already know, which is what free play is for.
- **The promotion rule stays simple.** `lastChapterOf` in `src/promotion.ts`
  takes a tier's last chapter. A seventh Pawn "chapter" in Lessons would move
  the promotion, or need a special case.
- **Free play has real content from day one,** not just your own levels and an
  empty board.
- **So players find it,** the promotion ceremony and the end of the Pawn tier
  in Lessons both point to it.
- **No lesson phase.** A challenge has a brief and **"Draws on" chips** naming
  the chapters and Codex entries it uses (say, `while` from Chapter 5 and
  slicing from Chapter 6). Each chip links to the chapter and to its Codex
  entry. The Codex lists everything the tier has taught. Hints, stars and
  "Show me a solution" work as on any level.

### What free play holds

The start menu's Free Play entry switches on.
- **Challenges:** the tier mastery sets, starting with the Pawn tier's (M4.5).
  For now these are free play's default levels.
- **My levels:** made in the editor (M4.2).
- **Shared with me:** levels opened from a link, a code or a file (M4.3).
- **Sandbox:** an open board to try things on, with every ability unlocked so
  far. The engine already builds such a board for lesson snippets
  (`levels.sandbox_level`).
- **Skins gallery:** the skins unlocked by promotions (`DESIGN.md` §8).
- **A later idea:** randomly generated puzzles, checked by the solver.

### Tags on every free-play level

Two tags *(Designer, 2026-10-07)*:
- **Tier:** what you need to know, such as "Pawn" for Chapters 1–6.
  - The engine works it out from what the level needs (its abilities, and the
    constructs its rules require) through `codex.history`, which already knows
    the chapter that teaches each one.
- **Difficulty:** five steps, each named by a Python keyword and colour-coded:

  | Step | Tag | Colour |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | `pass` | green |
  | 2 | `try` | yellow |
  | 3 | `raise` | orange |
  | 4 | `assert` | red |
  | 5 | `while True:` | flashing: a warning |

  - The author sets it: the designer for challenges (proposed in each spec,
    confirmed after play-testing), and the player for their own levels.
  - The editor helps by showing the solver's line count, the number of boards
    and the enemies.
- The lists filter and sort by both tags.

How the difficulty tag keeps to the look's rules (`DESIGN.md` §9), for M4.4's
spec:
- **Never colour alone.** The tag always shows its keyword (in the code font)
  and its step as five pips, so it reads in greyscale and to a screen reader.
- **New tokens:** its five colours are `light-dark()` tokens in
  `src/styles.css`, so the contrast check covers both themes.
- **The flashing:**
  - It stops with Animations reduced, leaving steady warning stripes.
  - It stays well under three flashes a second (the WCAG limit).
- **Off the board.** Red and the flashing stay on the tags, in lists and in
  the Challenge panel, never on the board, where red and magenta mean hostile.

### The format and the checker

- Level YAML gains `difficulty` (1 to 5).
- A challenge has no `lesson`; it keeps a brief.
- Challenges live in their own folder and listing, outside the curriculum in
  `chapters.yaml`.
- `engine/tests/test_levels.py` checks challenges like any level:
  - The reference solves every case and earns three stars.
  - The naive attempts fail as they say.
  - The hints are there.
  - A reference may call anything its tier teaches.

## M4.5 The Pawn tier's mastery set

From M3 *(Designer, 2026-09-26)*: at the end of each tier, a set of optional
mastery challenges covers the whole tier's concepts, in different ways. The
Pawn tier's comes in M4, after the editor.

*(Designer, 2026-10-07)*:
- **The set:**
  - 5 or 6 challenges, each mixing concepts from at least two chapters.
  - Each has a difficulty, spread from about `try` to `while True:`.
  - They live in Free Play → Challenges, as its default levels for now.
- **Chapter 1** is a prologue and needs no mastery challenge of its own. The
  set draws on it like any chapter.
- **No lesson phase** (M4.4).
- **No reward yet.** The designer has ideas for skins and trophies, for a later
  design and experience pass (`docs/QA.md`, "Revisit later").
- **Process, as in M3:**
  1. **Specs first,** written by Claude: what each trains, the board, the rules,
     par, the cases and a difficulty. Never solution text. The designer
     approves them.
  2. Claude builds the boards in the editor.
  3. References, naive attempts, hints and notes are written blind by a
     generator modelled on `scripts/gen_runes.py`, which prints only counts.
  4. A play-test, QA rounds, then the merge.
