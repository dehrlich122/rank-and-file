## What it is

Free Play → Challenges has had an empty set, "The Last Rank", since M4.4 ("Its challenges are coming soon"). M4.5
fills it: five challenges that mix what Chapters 1 to 6 taught, with no lesson phase, each with a difficulty tag
and "Draws on" chips. Solving them earns stars like any level. There is no reward yet (a later pass).

| In | Out |
|---|---|
| Five challenges in `levels/last-rank/`, references in `solutions/last-rank/` | A reward for finishing the set (skins, trophies: later) |
| A generator, `scripts/gen_mastery.py`, that writes them blind | Any engine change: levels are data. If a sketch needs one, the sketch changes |
| e2e checks for a challenge played for real (M4.4's checks used a stand-in) | New tiles, enemies or abilities |
| The hub and the Challenges page showing a filled set | The Knight tier |

## The set

Each challenge runs on several boards, so the code has to work out what it can't know ahead (the
level-design-necessity rule): a number, a place or a word differs from board to board. Described here by concept
and board mechanic only; no solution text anywhere in this file.

| # | Working title | Draws on | The board | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Toll Road | Ch 2, 4, 5 | A corridor, a different length on each board, with pits along it (none, one or two) and a way round each on the left. A guard at the end asks how far the piece walked. Count while walking; step round the gaps | 2 `try` |
| 2 | Leg by Leg | Ch 3, 5, 6 | A trail of runes on an open board. Each gives the next stretch of the route, one leg or several; how many runes there are isn't known | 3 `raise` |
| 3 | The Written Hour | Ch 2, 6 | A corridor to a timed gate. How often it opens is written on a rune, in words that have to be cut and turned into a number; the walk to the gate differs too, so the wait is arithmetic | 3 `raise` |
| 4 | The Long Gallery | Ch 4, 5 | A gallery of unknown length, three lanes wide, watched by rooks and by sentries that pace from wall to wall. At each square: capture, wait or walk on | 4 `assert` |
| 5 | The Last Rank | Ch 2 to 6 | The finale. A climb of unknown length past rooks to a guard who asks how many were taken; behind his gate a rune on the last rank says which way the goal is and how far; pits to bridge along the way, and nothing but the rune says where to stop | 5 `while True:` |

Every chapter from 2 to 6 is drawn on by at least two challenges; Chapter 1 (moving and turning) is in all of them.
*(Still true after the changes below: challenge 1 gave up Chapter 3 and challenge 2 took it.)*

**Sketches changed while building** (the rule in "How it is built", 3):

- **Challenge 1 draws on Ch 2, 4, 5, not 2, 3, 4** *(2026-11-16)*. For the guard's answer to differ between boards,
  the walk has to differ: the road's length and the number of pits. A way round a pit covers two squares of road in
  one pass, so the number of passes isn't known ahead, and a `for` over a fixed `range()` can't fit every board. The
  board asks for `while`. Forcing `for` would have meant a road that never comes back from its side step, where the
  pit is only a wall with another name.
- **Challenge 2 draws on Ch 3 as well, and some runes hold several legs** *(2026-11-16)*. With one leg to a rune it
  was Chapter 6's last level again with `while` for `for`. A rune that holds more than one leg asks for a loop
  inside the loop, which is Chapter 3's, and for cutting the text twice. The legs are words and a number ("left 3,
  right 10"), so the number's place in the text isn't fixed. It has no `look()` or `squares_ahead()`, and no two
  boards have the same size or start: reading the runes is the only way to find the goal (a sweep of the board
  can't be written to fit them all).
- **Challenge 4 has pacing sentries where the sketch had bishops** *(2026-11-16)*. The sketch's "wait" had nothing
  to wait for: rooks and bishops stand still, and `look()` can't tell an attacked square from a safe one, so a
  bishop could only be a hazard the code has no way to notice. A sentry (a patrol, which `look()` names) moves, so
  what the pawn sees changes from tick to tick: straight ahead it has to be waited for, diagonally ahead it can be
  taken. That is the loop that looks again after waiting, which no chapter level has. No turns in this one, so the
  only choices are the three in the sketch. Patrols are not new: Chapter 3 and the Testing ground have them.
- **Challenge 5's goal is "not shown" to the code, not to the player** *(2026-11-16)*. A level has `?` squares or
  several boards, not both, and the finale needs several boards. So the goal is drawn, but the pawn has no
  `at_goal()` and no `squares_ahead()` here, and the last rank runs on past the goal by a different amount on each
  board: only the rune's number says where to stop. That makes the walk a `for` over a number read from text, with
  a look for a pit on every pass, which is where Chapter 3 comes in.

## Decided without the designer (2026-11-16)

1. **The set's name stays "The Last Rank"** (M4.4's proposal; the id is `last-rank`). Its finale shares the name.
2. **Five challenges, not six.** Five gives one per difficulty step from `try` up, with two at `raise`. A sixth can
   be added later without changing anything else.
3. **Difficulties** as in the table: proposals, to be confirmed by the designer's play-test (`docs/M4/M4.md`).
   No challenge is tagged `pass`: the lessons' own mastery levels cover that step.
4. **Titles** are working titles. Renaming one is a one-line change in the generator.
5. **Boards come from the generator, not hand-painted in the editor.** `docs/M4/M4.md` says "Claude builds the
   boards in the editor"; the orchestrator cannot play the editor by hand, and Chapters 3 to 6 were all built by
   generators that probe each board with the engine. The files are the editor's own format, so any of them opens
   in the editor through "copy a level" if the designer wants to change one.
6. **Par** is each reference's own line count, as in the chapters.
7. **Hints** follow the hint-tiers rule: hint 1 points at what to notice, hint 2 explains the mechanic in words,
   hint 3 shows its code shape. No idle turns in hints or references.
8. **Small boards under a board wider than a chessboard** *(2026-11-17, QA-064)*. In a level where any board has
   more than 8 columns, the other boards sit in a row under the one on show instead of beside it, for every board
   of that level. The finale's boards (9 to 12 columns) were being drawn at about 23px a square. Seven levels
   change: challenges 2 and 5 and five lesson levels with boards that wide (`ch02-l06`, `ch03-l02`, `ch06-l04`
   to `-l06`); the other twenty levels with several boards keep the layout the designer verified in QA-032.
   *(First built as "more columns than rows"; that also moved the small boards on six lesson levels with boards
   such as 7 × 5, where nothing was hard to read, and missed Chapter 6's big square boards, where it was. So it
   was narrowed the same day to the thing the finding was about: the board's width.)*
9. **No `/simplify` pipeline for M4.5** *(2026-11-17)*. Outside level content and its generator the branch holds
   e2e checks and the three small fixes of the QA round (QA-063 to QA-065), each with a check. The pipeline's cost
   (a lead and workers) is out of proportion to that; the full e2e and `npm run check` judge it.

**For the designer** (none blocks the build): 1 (the name), 3 (the difficulties, after playing), 4 (the titles),
8 (the wide boards' layout: look at challenge 5).

## How it is built

**Content stays with the orchestrator** (`docs/WORKFLOW.md`, "Level content"): no level, hint, reference or note
goes to an agent or appears in a conversation.

1. **The generator** `scripts/gen_mastery.py`, modelled on `scripts/levelgen.py` (`build`, `main`, `said`) and
   `scripts/gen_runes.py`: per challenge a skeleton of fixed lines (kept in ROT13 in the script) with holes the
   engine fills by search; boards built from a spec and probed (`probe`) before anything is written; hints 1 and
   2 and the notes in ROT13, checked by counting unknown words against the repo's vocabulary. It prints counts
   only. It also writes the set's entry in `levels/challenges.yaml`.
2. **Each challenge is accepted by the engine's own checks** (`engine/tests/test_levels.py`): the reference solves
   every board with three stars, each naive attempt fails as it says, the hints are there, the reference calls
   nothing the Pawn tier hasn't taught, and the worked-out tier is the set's ("Pawn").
3. **One at a time,** easiest first: generate, `npm run check`, commit. A sketch that the engine can't express, or
   whose boards don't force the idea, is changed here in the spec first (with the reason), never by an engine change.

## Hand-test list

1. Free Play → Challenges: "The Last Rank" lists five challenges, each with a difficulty tag (keyword and pips) and
   the Pawn tier tag. The Difficulty filter and the sort work on them.
2. Open each challenge: a brief, "Draws on" chips that link to their chapters, no Learn tab, the Codex listing the
   whole Pawn tier.
3. Play each one. For each: is it solvable from the brief and the board? Is the difficulty tag fair? Does it need
   the ideas its chips name, or is there a shortcut that skips them?
4. Hints read in order: notice, then the mechanic in words, then its code shape.
5. After a solve: stars, the comparison with the reference, "Next challenge →"; after the fifth, "Back to Free Play".
6. Lessons and the promotion screen still point to the set, and it no longer says "coming soon".
7. The QA round's three fixes: the retest list at the end of `docs/QA.md` Session 18 (the solved card fits; the
   small boards under a wide board, on challenges 2 and 5 and on five lesson levels; `bridge()` shown as code).

## As built

*(filled in as it lands)*

- **The generator** `scripts/gen_mastery.py`. `scripts/levelgen.py` now writes a challenge too: a spec with `set`
  gets `set`, `difficulty` and `draws_on` in place of `chapter` and `lesson`, and its files go in the set's folder
  (`placing`, `folder`). The older generators' output is unchanged (`gen_runes.py` and `gen_conditions.py` rerun:
  no diff). Decoded hints and notes are checked word by word against the words of `docs/`, `lessons/`, `DESIGN.md`
  and `CLAUDE.md`; only the number of strangers is printed (`strangers`).
- **Challenge 1, "The Toll Road"** (`last-rank-01`): four boards (roads of 5 to 9 squares; none, one or two pits,
  three squares of floor or more between two pits so the ways round stay apart and the route is forced). Its
  reference reuses `gen_while.py`'s search over one loop and a chain of branches; the count for a way round is a
  hole the engine fills (1 to 6 tried). The search finds one program, 16 lines (the par). Each board's own typed
  route solves that board only. Three wrong attempts: typed, the loop as a single check, every pass counted as one.
- **Challenge 2, "Leg by Leg"** (`last-rank-02`): four boards (trails of two to five runes, a rune of one to three
  legs, one leg of two digits). The reference is a fixed skeleton with four holes (the outer loop's condition, where
  the text is cut, the test for the turn, how the number is taken); 30 of the 72 fillings solve every board and two
  boards the level doesn't have, and the first in the lessons' order is kept. Par 8. Three wrong attempts: typed,
  the outer loop as a single check, each rune taken as one leg.
- **Challenge 3, "The Written Hour"** (`last-rank-03`): four boards (a gate open for the first two ticks of every
  4, 5, 7 or 12; a walk of 3 to 8 squares). One open tick can't be passed at all (the gate shuts on the pawn), so
  it is two: the engine said so in `probe`. On two boards the walk is longer than the gate's cycle, so the wait
  needs the remainder, not a plain subtraction. No `look()`, so the gate can't be watched until it opens; no turns.
  The skeleton's holes are which piece of the text is the number, the form of the wait and two small numbers the
  engine settles; 4 of 60 fillings solve every board and four the level doesn't have. Par 5. Three wrong attempts:
  typed, the wait without the remainder, the number's first character only.
- **Challenge 4, "The Long Gallery"** (`last-rank-04`): four boards, 7 to 11 ranks long, written as a list of events
  (`gallery`): empty ranks, a rook diagonally ahead on one side (a wall under it closes its file below, as in
  Chapter 4's mastery level), a sentry pacing the next rank. Each sentry is set off so that the pawn finds it where
  the board's design says: diagonally ahead on three meetings, straight ahead on three. The reference is a fixed
  loop; of four variants tried on every board and two the level doesn't have, the two orders of the sides solve and
  the two with a branch missing don't. Par 9. Three wrong attempts: typed, no waiting, rooks only.
- **Challenge 5, "The Last Rank"** (`last-rank-05`): four boards (`last_rank`: the gallery's `climb` with one to
  four rooks, a guard's gate, then the last rank with a rune, a goal 3 to 6 squares west or east, none to two pits
  and two planks carried). The skeleton's holes are the question that picks the turn and which piece of the text is
  the number; 4 of 8 fillings solve every board and two the level doesn't have. Par 21. Three wrong attempts:
  typed, one side's rooks not counted, no look before a step.
- **The e2e with a filled set** (M4.5-3, `scripts/e2e/free.check.mjs`). The full run at `2af25ef`, before the
  package: 298/300, the two failures being M4.4's checks that assumed an empty set (the "coming soon" line; "no
  filter row on Challenges" in QA-049's check). Both are rewritten, and four checks are new, in the section "M4.5:
  The Last Rank, a challenge played for real": the list (five rows, tags, "0 of 5 solved", the filter row); the
  Difficulty and Tier filters and both sorts, with the difficulties read from the rows so a retagged challenge
  breaks nothing; a challenge's screen (brief, tier and difficulty, "Draws on" chips that lead to their lessons, no
  Learn tab); a solve with the reference from disk (three stars, "Next challenge →", "1 of 5 solved" on the list
  and the hub and over a reload, "Back to Free Play" after the last); hints in order and "Show me a solution" on
  the third failed run, equal to the file. No check carries a challenge's difficulty, a hint's text or code. The
  checks found nothing wrong in the app.
- **The QA round** *(automated, 2026-11-17; `docs/QA.md` Session 18)*. One worker drove the filled set's screens
  in headless Edge at two window sizes in both themes, without opening a hint or a solution. Seven findings.
  Three are fixed, each with its e2e check (M4.5-4 and -4b):
  - **QA-063:** a solved card's head row wraps, so the card and "Next challenge →" fit the board's column.
  - **QA-064:** in a level with a board wider than 8 columns the small boards go in a row under the board on
    show (decision 8). `mountLevel` sets `boards-wide` on `.boards` from the board sizes the engine reports; in
    `styles.css` the row-under layout is the base and "beside" the exception above 700px.
  - **QA-065:** the Challenge panel's Goal, Obstacles, Rules and Stars lists show a code span as code (`withCode`).
  Left for the designer with no change (QA.md has the detail): a narrow window plays the run out of sight (older,
  every level); the wait for a verdict over four boards; "the Codex has everything" against a challenge's Codex;
  three small things on the Challenges list.
- **All five** regenerate byte for byte from `scripts/gen_mastery.py` (a full run leaves `git status` clean).
  Still to do in the feature-complete pass: nobody has played them. The boards have only been read through
  `scripts.show_level` for challenge 1; the designer's play-test is the first look at the other four.
