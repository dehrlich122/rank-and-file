# M5.1 · The knight as a piece: spec

**Status: approved *(Designer, 2026-11-17: "approve the recommendations")*: gate 1 passed. Building. The next
stop is the designer's picks from the design sheet, then QA1.**

M5.1 gives the game its second piece. Until now "knight" was a skin over a pawn; from here a level can say its
piece is a knight, and the knight has its own rules. The tier's plan and the decisions this builds on are in
`docs/M5/M5.md` (decisions 1 to 4 and 10, all *(Designer, 2026-11-17)*). Branch: `m5-1-knight`.

## What it is

| In | Out |
|---|---|
| A `Knight` in the engine: `jump`, `look`, `position`, `at_goal`, `wait`, `read` | Chapter 7 and its levels (M5.2) |
| The jump's rules, its look on the board (long leg, short leg, the turn), its sound | The examiner, and anything about functions (M5.2) |
| The squares the knight can land on, drawn on the board | Keys, doors, the satchel (M5.3) |
| "Auto" as the default in Settings → Piece | The bishop (M5.4) |
| The knight's Codex entries, autocomplete, hover help, errors in its own words | The editor building knight levels (M5.5) |
| Three practice levels in the Testing ground, with a lesson that teaches the jump | A change to any Pawn-tier level |
| A design sheet first: the knight's sprite, the jump's motion, the landing squares | |

## What the investigation found *(M5.1-0, an Opus lead, read-only, 2026-11-17)*

The app is in better shape for a second piece than the 225 mentions of "pawn" suggested:

- **The engine is mostly generic already.** Pieces are a registry; a level names its piece; nearly every message
  the world gives uses the level's piece name; the rules checker and the scratch REPL don't care which piece it is.
- **Landing is already one function.** Whatever happens when the pawn arrives on a square (waypoints, gems, pits,
  being caught) is in one place and a jump reuses it. One action is already one tick for every clock.
- **The knight's art exists** in all four facings (the Charger skin).
- **The squares an enemy attacks** travel from the engine to the board in the world's state; the landing squares
  can travel the same way, so the board draws them without knowing a knight's rules.

What does assume a pawn, and is M5.1's work:

- **The engine:** a handful of messages ("Pawns never step backwards!", "your pawn isn't standing on a rune",
  "walk off the edge"), `help()`'s introduction, and the goal and obstacle descriptions that say "walking" and
  "passing over".
- **A knight asked to walk** would get a flat "doesn't have an ability called `move`". It needs its own kind
  answer.
- **Lesson snippets always run a pawn.** The piece isn't passed along the path from the lesson to the engine, so
  a knight lesson's snippets couldn't run a knight today.
- **The skin is chosen without looking at the level,** and stored settings can't tell "chose the pawn" from
  "never chose".
- **The board moves the hero in one straight slide** and draws one straight trail; sound has no jump.
- **The variables panel and one line of the level screen** look for a `Pawn` by name.
- **The editor's solvability check** is built on walking. That is M5.5's, listed there.

The lead ran out of room before opening the autocomplete and hover-help code and the e2e suites. Both are covered
below: package M5.1-4b checks the first with a real two-argument ability, and the full `npm run e2e` at
feature-complete shows what a second piece breaks.

## Decisions *(Designer, 2026-11-17: all six recommendations approved)*

Decision 3's two versions still go on the design sheet, where the mark itself is picked.

| # | Question | Decided (as recommended) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Landing on an armoured enemy** (one that can't be taken) | An error, in the pawn's words for it: the run stops and says this one can't be taken. It isn't shown as a landing square | The same answer the pawn gets, so one rule to learn. A lost run would punish a mistake the board could have shown |
| 2 | **What a jump touches** | Only where it lands. A waypoint, gem or plank the knight flies over isn't crossed or picked up | It follows from "only the landing square matters" (M5 decision 3). The level text for waypoints changes from "passing over it is enough" to "land on it" on knight levels |
| 3 | **Which landing squares the board shows** | Only the ones it could land on without an error, updated as the run steps (a gate that opens adds its square). A pit or an attacked square is still shown: landing there is allowed, and deadly, and its own mark already says so. **Both versions go on the design sheet** | The marks answer "where can I go?"; a mark on a wall would say the wrong thing |
| 4 | **Players who already have a saved Piece setting** | A saved "pawn" becomes "Auto" once; a saved "knight" stays | Until today "pawn" was the default, so almost nobody chose it. On Pawn-tier levels Auto looks the same; on knight levels it shows the knight |
| 5 | **The skins gallery** | An "Auto" card comes first: "each level's own piece". It is the one marked as worn until a skin is chosen | Otherwise nothing in the gallery looks selected |
| 6 | **Does the Codex treat the knight's `wait()`, `read()`, `at_goal()` and `position` as new?** | Yes: they appear as the knight's own entries on its first level, short, saying "as the pawn's" | The object in the code changed name; `help(knight.wait)` has to answer |

Not questions, noted so they aren't lost:

- **Guards hear only a piece standing next to their gate.** A knight has to *land* beside one. Chapter 7's lessons
  say so wherever a guard appears.
- **A knight level built in the editor would be tagged "Pawn tier"** until a Chapter 7 level exists to say where
  the knight comes in. The editor can't build one before M5.5, and Chapter 7 arrives in M5.2, so it fixes itself.
- **For Chapter 8:** an inventory and doors fit beside what exists (planks carried, gates opened). Nothing in
  M5.1 has to change for them.

## The practice levels *(Testing ground; written blind by the orchestrator)*

Three short levels, each with a lesson, hints and a reference, from a new generator `scripts/gen_knight.py`:

| # | Trains | The board |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The jump: `knight.jump(dx, dy)`, and what the two numbers mean | An open board, the goal a few jumps away. The lesson shows a jump, a jump that isn't an L, and asking a knight to walk |
| 2 | Over, and onto: a jump passes over anything and takes what it lands on | Walls no pawn could pass, an enemy standing on the way. The lesson shows a landing on a wall and a landing in a pit |
| 3 | Looking before landing: `knight.look(dx, dy)` | What is on the landing squares differs between the level's boards, so the code has to look and choose |

They are the checkpoint of M5 decision 10: after playing them, QA1 asks whether decisions 1 to 3 still hold.

## How it is built

**The contract, committed before any package** (names only; an Opus lead writes it as stubs and types):

- Engine: `Knight(Piece)` with `jump(dx, dy)`, `look(dx, dy)`, `at_goal()`, `position`, `wait(ticks=1)`, `read()`;
  `PIECES` gains it. `World.jump`, `World.look_at`, `World.landings`. Two new errors: one for a jump that isn't
  an L, one for asking a piece for another piece's ability ("Knights don't walk: they jump").
- **The engine reports the look.** A jump is one `jump` event carrying where it started, the corner after the long
  leg, and what it took; the state after it has the knight facing along the short leg. The world's state gains
  the landing squares on knight levels only. The UI draws these and works nothing out.
- `protocol.ts` mirrors it; the lesson-snippet path (`bridge.py`, `worker.ts`, `lesson.ts`) carries the piece.
- Settings: the Piece choice becomes "auto" or a skin; the skin worn is worked out from the choice and the level.

## As built *(so far)*

- **M5.1-C built the jump's rules as well as the contract** (2026-11-17). A stubbed world would have failed the
  checks, and the real rules came to about 60 lines on helpers that existed. So **M5.1-1a is folded into C**;
  M5.1-1b (the wording) stands. What is committed:
  - `Knight` in `PIECES` with `jump(dx, dy)`, `look(dx, dy)`, `at_goal()`, `position`, `wait(ticks=1)`, `read()`.
    A bad offset is refused by the piece before the world is asked, so Scratch Python's stand-in says so too.
  - `World.jump`, `World.look_at`, `World.landings`, `KNIGHT_JUMPS`; one rule (`_landing_error`) decides both
    whether a jump lands and which squares are landing squares.
  - `JumpError` (also a `TypeError`) and `WrongPieceError` (also an `AttributeError`), with `Piece.CANT`: each of
    the pawn's eight abilities asked of a knight answers "Knights don't walk: they jump."
  - The `jump` event carries `from`, `via` (the corner after the long leg) and `took`; the state after it faces
    along the short leg. The state carries `landings` on knight levels only, **empty once the run is lost**
    (the lead's choice; for the designer to confirm at QA1).
  - `protocol.ts` mirrors it, with `piece?` on the two sandbox requests.
  - A player could notice: `help("knight.jump")`, typed as text on a pawn level, now answers. M5.1-1b puts that
    back.
- **M5.1-D, the design sheet:** `design/knight-piece.html`, shots in `docs/M5/M5.1/shots/`
  (`node design/shoot-knight-piece.mjs`). Takes: N1 to N3 (the north-facing sprite), J1 and J2 (slide, hop) with
  R (motion reduced), T (the trail), M1 to M3 (landing marks) each as V1 and V2, S (Settings) and G (the
  gallery). A jump takes 378 ms at 1× and 94.5 ms at 4×, split 60/40 between the long and the short leg.
- **M5.1-1b, the wording** (2026-11-17): on a knight level, errors, `help()` and the goal and obstacle text name
  the knight and speak of landing; pawn levels are pinned character for character.
  - `help` is now built per run and finds the level's piece in the player's namespace; help by text answers only
    for that piece (so `help("knight.jump")` on a pawn level is unknown again).
  - Beyond the brief, and right: pits, attacked squares and enemies are described for a piece that lands.
  - **By decision 6, the knight's `at_goal`, `position`, `wait` and `read` entries say "just like the pawn's".**
    So a knight level's Codex does mention the pawn there, on purpose.
  - Found, and given to M5.1-2: Scratch Python keeps the previous level's stand-in piece when the piece changes.
- **M5.1-2** (2026-11-17): a lesson's snippets run the level's piece; Scratch Python drops the previous level's
  stand-in when the piece changes, unless the player gave that name a value of their own.
- **The review of M5.1-1b** (an Opus reviewer, 2026-11-17) found seven things. **Package M5.1-1c fixes 1, 2, 3, 5
  and 7** (owns `codex.py`, `errors.py`, `repl.py`, the one line in `runner.py`, and their tests):
  1. `codex.py` `player_namespace`: the per-run `help` keeps the wrong qualified name, so **on a pawn level**
     `help(print, len)` lost its friendly "takes at most 1 value" answer, `print(help)` shows an internal name,
     and `help.__doc__` says the wrong thing. Fix: carry the name and a player-facing docstring over
     (`functools.wraps`), with a pinned test for `help(print, len)`. *A regression on Pawn-tier levels: fix first.*
  2. `runner.py` about 225 (unmet objectives): a knight level still says "you didn't cross the waypoint" where

     `Knight` with a `jump` method would trigger it. Contrived; leave it, with a comment saying so.
  5. The module-level `codex.help` is no longer called by anything: move its docstring to the per-run one and
     drop it.
  6. "This piece lands rather than walks" is now written in five places, each comparing against "knight"

## Hand-test list *(draft, for QA1)*

1. Open the Testing ground: three knight levels are there, and each shows a knight without a visit to Settings.
2. Jump in all eight L shapes. Each plays as two legs and the knight ends facing along the short one.
3. Jump over a wall. Land on an enemy and take it. Land in a pit and lose.
4. Try a jump that isn't an L, and try `knight.move()`: each gets a kind, exact error.
5. The landing squares show before the run, move with the knight while stepping, and rewind correctly.
6. `help(knight)`, `help(knight.jump)`, the Codex tab, hover help and autocomplete all know the knight.
7. The lesson's snippets run a knight, including the ones that go wrong.
8. Settings → Piece: Auto is selected; choosing Pawn or Charger overrides it everywhere; Reset progress returns
   to Auto.
9. Every Pawn-tier level plays and looks exactly as before, with sound.
10. **The checkpoint:** after playing, do offsets, a knight that only jumps, and the landing rules still feel
    right?
