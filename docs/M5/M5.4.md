# M5.4 · Promotion to bishop and the Knight tier's mastery set: spec

**Status: approved *(Designer, 2026-11-27: "approved")*: gate 1 passed, all ten decisions as recommended.
Building; the bishop is picked (B1, with a taller mitre); the next stop is QA1.** Written 2026-11-27. Branch:
`m5-4-promotion-and-mastery`, from `main` at `225792a` (M5.3 merged and published).

M5.4 closes the Knight tier for a player: the promotion from knight to bishop when Chapter 8's core levels are
solved, the bishop as a skin, and five challenges in Free Play that mix Chapters 7 and 8 with the Pawn tier. It
builds on `docs/M5/M5.md` (decision 11 and the M5.4 outline, *(Designer, 2026-11-17)*), on `DESIGN.md` §8, and
on the two things it copies: the first promotion (M3.7) and the Pawn tier's set, "The Last Rank" (M4.5).

*(Recommended)* marks a recommendation that waits for the designer's answer. Challenges are described by what
they practise and their board mechanic, never by how to solve them.

## What it is

| In | Out |
|---|---|
| The ceremony's second row: knight to bishop, once, when Chapter 8's five core levels are solved; "watch again" on Chapter 8 | The Bishop tier itself (M6): how a bishop moves, its chapters, a level whose piece is a bishop |
| The bishop as a skin: on a design sheet first, then in the ceremony, Free Play → Skins and Settings → Piece | A reward for finishing a mastery set (the experience pass, after M5) |
| Five challenges in a new set in Free Play → Challenges, written blind by a generator, with e2e checks for a challenge played for real | Any engine change: challenges are data. If a sketch needs one, the sketch changes |
| The words: the ceremony's headline and teaser, the skin's name, the set's name and titles | The level editor learning the knight, keys and doors (M5.5) |

## What is already there *(read from the code and the earlier specs, 2026-11-27; no agent)*

- **A promotion is a row of data** (`src/promotion.ts`, `PROMOTIONS`): the tier, the piece it goes from and to, a
  headline and a teaser. The screen (`src/ui/promotionScreen.ts`, `#/promotion/<tier>`), the "seen once" record,
  "watch again" in Lessons, the sound, and the rule that a skin is unlocked by its tier's last chapter are all
  written for any row. What they need for a second row is the piece itself: `Piece` is "pawn" or "knight" today
  (`src/settings.ts`), and a hero's drawing exists for those two only.
- **A bishop is already in the game, as an enemy:** a lathe body and a mitre with a slanted cut, drawn in broken
  magenta wire like every enemy (`src/ui/sprites/`). The player's bishop must never be mistaken for it.
- **Under "Auto" a level shows its own piece** *(Designer, 2026-11-17)*. No level's piece is a bishop until M6, so
  until then the bishop is seen only when the player chooses it, and it moves as that level's piece moves (a
  skin is a look, `DESIGN.md` §8).
- **A challenge set is a row of data too** (`levels/challenges.yaml`, read by `src/content.ts`): a set has a tier,
  and the Challenges page groups, filters and sorts by what the sets say. Nothing found that assumes one set. It
  has only ever been run with one, so the first package that touches it says what it found.
- **Guards, runes, keys, doors and the examiner all work for the knight** (Chapters 7 and 8 use them).
- **`knight.look(dx, dy)` has never been in a lesson.** It is a Testing-ground ability (M5.2: "until a chapter
  needs it"; Chapter 8 didn't). By the rule for mastery sets (M5 decision 11: a challenge practises, it never
  teaches) no challenge may need it. So a challenge's code can't see a square before landing on it: what differs
  between boards has to reach the code as an argument, a rune, a guard's question or the satchel.

## Decisions *(Designer, 2026-11-27: all ten as recommended)*

### 1. The bishop skin's look: three takes on a sheet, the designer picks

**Picked: B1, with a taller mitre** *(Designer, 2026-11-27: "B1 is my preference. give it the taller mitre too
to better distinguish it from the pawn.")* The taller mitre is drawn on the sheet first (M5.4-D2) and goes into
the game from there; the designer sees it at QA1, or on the sheet before.

As the knight got in M3.7 (three versions drawn, the Charger picked). `design/bishop.html` shows each take:

- in all four facings at board size (the Charger's lesson, `docs/QA.md`: facing north it read as "a narrow
  spire" that looked little like its card), in both themes;
- large, as the ceremony and the Skins card show it;
- on a board beside the enemy bishop and beside the Charger and the pawn, to judge that it reads as the hero and
  as one of the family.

The three directions *(Recommended as the set to draw; say if one should be swapped)*:

| Take | The idea | A name that fits it |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | The chess bishop made the hero's: a tall mitre with the slanted cut lit from inside, a collar, the solid body and lit eyes the pawn and the Charger have | Prelate |
| B2 | A hooded figure: the mitre's cut becomes one lit slit under a cowl, a narrow robe that widens to the base | Oracle |
| B3 | The diagonal made visible: a faceted spire that leans, with one band of light running corner to corner across it | Envoy |

The sheet is the first package after gate 1. **The pick is a short stop of its own**, as in M5.2 and M5.3; the
challenges are written meanwhile, since nothing in them waits for it.

### 2. The skin's name

**"Prelate", the name B1 carries,** until the designer says otherwise (the pick named the take and not the
word; it is one line of data). Settings → Piece will say "Bishop · Prelate".

The pawn's skin is "Pawn", the knight's is "Charger" (Settings → Piece says "Knight · Charger").
*(Recommended)* **The name comes with the pick:** each take carries the name it suggests (the table above), and
the designer may keep the look and change the word. It is one line of data.

### 3. When the bishop can be worn

- **A. From the promotion** *(Recommended)*: the skin unlocks when Chapter 8's core levels are solved, as
  `DESIGN.md` §8 says every skin does. Chosen in Settings → Piece or on the ceremony, it is drawn on every level,
  jumping on knight levels and walking on pawn levels. "Auto" never shows it until M6 has bishop levels.
- B. Not until M6: the ceremony shows the bishop as a preview and the skin stays locked until the Bishop tier
  exists.

A is what the ceremony promises ("shows the new piece"), and it is the only thing a promotion can hand over
before M6. What it costs: a bishop that jumps in an L. That is the rule the designer set for the Charger on
pawn levels (a skin never changes what a piece does or what the code calls it).

### 4. The ceremony's words

The first row says "Promoted: pawn to knight" and "The knight moves in an L, over anything in its way. Chapter 7
is waiting." For the second *(Recommended)*:

- **Headline:** "Promoted: knight to bishop"
- **Teaser:** "The bishop slides along the diagonals, as far as the way is clear. Its chapters aren't built yet:
  the skin is yours now, and the Knight tier's challenges are waiting in Free Play."

It says honestly that the Bishop tier isn't built (the M5 plan asks for that). When M6 ships its first chapter
the teaser changes to name it, one line.

"What the tier taught" is the two chapters' own summaries, as in the first ceremony. No new words.

### 5. Where the ceremony sends the player

The first ceremony leads on to Chapter 7. There is no Chapter 9. *(Recommended)* The way on is **the Knight
tier's set in Free Play → Challenges**, with Lessons as the other button, and "Wear the bishop skin" as it is.
If the set isn't shipped in the same PR this would point at nothing: both halves of M5.4 ship together.

### 6. The set's name

The Pawn tier's set is "The Last Rank" (where a pawn is promoted), and its finale shares the name.

- **A. "The Knight's Tour"** *(Recommended)*: the oldest knight puzzle there is (visit every square once). The
  finale shares the name. Id `knights-tour`.
- B. "The Fork": what a knight is feared for in chess. Short, but it promises two enemies attacked at once,
  which no challenge is about.
- C. "Outpost": a knight's strong square. Reads as a place, like "The Last Rank"; means little to a non-player.

### 7. Five challenges, one per step from `try` to `while True:`

*(Recommended)* The Pawn set's shape: five, tagged `try`, `raise`, `raise`, `assert`, `while True:`. None is
tagged `pass` (each chapter's own mastery level covers that step). The tags are proposals until the designer
has played them. A sixth can be added later without changing anything else.

### 8. The five sketches

"The set", below. *(Recommended as a set.)* The designer approves what each practises, its board and what
differs between its boards or calls; par is each reference's own line count, as everywhere. A sketch that the
engine can't express, or whose boards don't force the idea, is changed here first, with the reason (M4.5's
rule).

### 9. Process: a small contract, and `/simplify` by size

- **The contract is one line and one row** (`Piece` gains "bishop"; `PROMOTIONS` gains a row). `docs/WORKFLOW.md`
  has an Opus agent write every contract. *(Recommended)* Here the package that brings the skin into the game
  types it, with no lead: there is nothing to design.
- **`/simplify`:** *(Recommended)* the full pipeline only if the diff outside level content, the generator and
  `design/` is more than about 300 lines; under that, one `reviewer` on the diff after the free checks (M4.5
  took the same line: its code diff was three small fixes).

### 10. One old e2e check, while we are here

The `app` check "M2: Esc closes the comparison…" reads the page a fixed 100 ms after the key. It failed under
load in M5.2 and against an eight-day-old dev server in M5.3. *(Recommended)* One small package makes it wait
for the dialog to go instead, and reports honestly if Esc is ever really lost.

## The set *(decision 8; approved as a set, Designer, 2026-11-27)*

Working titles. Each challenge is solved by a program of the player's own on several boards, or by a function
the examiner calls several times, so the code has to work out what it can't know ahead (the real-necessity
rule). Three board shapes carry over from the chapters and keep the knight's geometry out of the puzzle: the
**ladder** (every jump the same distance up, one step left or right), the **hub** (stones one jump from a
middle stone: out, and back) and the **stride** (a short figure of jumps that repeats).

Every challenge's abilities are `jump` and what its row lists. None has `look`.

| # | Working title | Draws on (the lesson that taught each thing) | The board, and what differs | How it is judged | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Written Climb | `if`/`elif` (Ch 4); `split()` (Ch 6); `read()` on a rune the knight stands on (7.4); a list and `for` over it (8.1) | A ladder. The way up is written in words on a rune at its foot, and no two boards have the same way or the same height | The player's program, on several boards | 2 `try` |
| 2 | Out and Back | counting with a loop (Ch 3, Ch 5); a function with a parameter (7.3); pairs and unpacking (8.2); `pick_up()` and a door (8.3); a list built with `append` (8.4) | A winding trail of stones out to a key, and a door beside the start that the key opens. The examiner hands the way out as a list of jumps; nobody hands the way back, and the trail is the only ground there is | The examiner calls the player's function with a different trail each time | 3 `raise` |
| 3 | The Long Stair | `while` (Ch 5); `int()` and cutting text (Ch 6); a number kept across the program, the guard's answer (Ch 2); parameters (7.3); a function that hands a value back, and scope (7.4, 7.5); functions that call functions (7.6) | A stair of flights. Each flight is one stride repeated; how many times is written on a rune at the flight's foot, in a sentence. How many flights there are isn't known. A guard at the top asks how many strides were made in all | The player's program, on several boards | 3 `raise` |
| 4 | Roll Call | cutting text, `int()` (Ch 6); `return` (7.4); places as pairs, a jump worked out from two places (8.2); `append` (8.4); `sorted()`, and that pairs order by their first value (8.5) | Stones in a field, each with a rune that holds a name and a number. The examiner hands the stones as a list of places and wants the names back, ordered by their numbers. The names, the numbers, the places and how many there are all differ | The examiner calls the player's function and judges what it hands back | 4 `assert` |
| 5 | The Knight's Tour | Ch 4 to 8: everything above, with `inventory`, `in`, `min()` or `max()` (8.3, 8.5) and a default value (7.3) | The finale, in three parts. A hub with numbered keys, a different handful on each board. A guard who asks about them. Behind his gate a ladder whose way up is written on a rune, with doors on some rungs that open only to keys the board happened to hold, so the climb depends on the satchel | The player's program, on several boards, under a line limit that asks for functions | 5 `while True:` |

- **Every lesson from 7.2 to 8.5 is drawn on by at least one challenge,** Chapters 7 and 8 by all five, and each
  Pawn-tier chapter from 2 to 6 by at least one. Chapter 1 (calling, arguments) is in all of them.
- **Each challenge's wrong attempts** (written with the references, blind): the route or the answer written out
  for one board fails on the others; the idea the challenge is about, left out, ends as the board says it must
  (a locked door, a pit, a guard who isn't answered, the examiner's "wanted … got …").
- **Help works as everywhere** (decision 11): three hints (notice; the mechanic in words; its code shape), and
  "Show me a solution" after three failed runs. No idle turns in hints or references.
- **What may change while building:** challenge 5's doors need the code to know a door's name without `look`;
  if the boards can't make that fair, its third part becomes a second guard. Challenge 2 and 8.6 both use a
  trail and a hub; the designer hasn't yet said whether 8.6's hub reads, and the finale leans on a hub too.

## Hand-test list *(draft, for QA1; it grows as the build goes)*

1. Solve the last of Chapter 8's five core levels (or, with all five solved and the ceremony not yet seen, any
   core level of Chapter 7 or 8: it follows a solve in its own tier, `levelView.ts`): after the celebration the
   ceremony plays once, knight to bishop, with what the tier taught and the teaser. Reload: it doesn't come back
   by itself.
2. Lessons → Chapter 8 shows "watch again", and it replays the ceremony. Chapter 6's still replays the first.
3. On the ceremony, "Wear the bishop skin": a knight level and a pawn level both show the bishop, in all four
   facings; the code still says `knight.` or `pawn.`; "switch back to Auto" puts each level's own piece back.
4. Free Play → Skins has three cards; Settings → Piece lists Auto, Pawn, Knight · Charger and the bishop by its
   name. Before the promotion the bishop is locked in both. Reset progress locks it again and Settings → Piece
   falls back.
5. On a board with an enemy bishop, the worn bishop can't be mistaken for it.
6. The ceremony's way on opens the Knight tier's set: Challenges with the Tier filter on "Knight" (built this
   way in package 2, see "As built"; say if a filter set for you is unwelcome). The Pawn ceremony's link and
   the two "Next: … challenges" links in Lessons do the same for their tier.
7. Free Play → Challenges lists two sets. The new one has five challenges with their difficulty tags and the
   Knight tier tag; the tier filter and the sort work across both sets. "The Last Rank" is as it was.
8. Open each challenge: a brief, "Draws on" chips that link to their chapters, no Learn tab, the Codex listing
   the Pawn and Knight tiers.
9. Play each one. Is it solvable from the brief and the board? Is its tag fair? Does it need the ideas its
   chips name, or is there a shortcut round them? Does anything in it have to be learned there?
10. Hints read in order; after three failed runs "Show me a solution" is offered; after a solve, the stars, the
    comparison and "Next challenge →", and after the fifth "Back to Free Play".
11. Every Pawn-tier level and Chapters 7 and 8 play exactly as before.

## How it is built

**Level content stays with the orchestrator** (`docs/WORKFLOW.md`, "Level content"): no challenge, hint,
reference or note goes to an agent or appears in a conversation. A new generator, `scripts/gen_tour.py`,
modelled on `scripts/gen_mastery.py` and `scripts/gen_satchel.py`: skeletons kept unreadable in the script,
boards probed by the engine before anything is written, counts printed and nothing else. Each challenge is
accepted by `engine/tests/test_levels.py` (the reference solves every board or call with three stars, each
wrong attempt fails as it says, nothing is called that the tier hasn't taught, the worked-out tier is "Knight").
One challenge at a time, easiest first: generate, `npm run check`, commit.

**The skin** goes sheet → pick → game, in that order, and nothing reaches the game before the pick.
