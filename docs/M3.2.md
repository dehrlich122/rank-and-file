# M3.2 · Chapter 2, Counting Steps: level specs

Branch `m3-2-chapter-2`. This was **checkpoint 1**: the designer approves
these specs before anything is built. The decisions they build on are in
`docs/M3.md` → Chapter 2.

**Approved** *(Designer, 2026-09-29)*: the specs as written, the six
proposed changes, and the recommendation on each open question. What was
built is under "Built" at the end.

The specs give each level's concept, map, rules, par and boards. They never
give solution code. References, wrong attempts, hints and solution notes
are written blind when the levels are built. Every map below was checked
with the real engine: each board parses and is solvable. Par is
provisional until the reference solution is written.

## What Chapter 2 teaches, and what makes each part necessary

| Level | Trains | What makes it necessary |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 The Surveyor's Road | variables: name a number, then reuse the name | only one number in the code, written once, plus a line limit (QA-029) |
| 2.2 Doing the Sums | a function that gives back a value (`squares_ahead()`), and `+`, `-`, `*` on it | three boards; the goal is placed by a rule the brief states (QA-029) |
| 2.3 Halfway There | a function that gives back a value (`squares_ahead()`), and `//` | three boards of different sizes |
| 2.4 Halt! | f-strings: putting values into text | a guard whose answer differs on each board, and a rule to answer with an f-string |
| 2.5 Keeping Time | the remainder operator `%`: working with cycles | a timed gate, on four boards that each need a different wait |
| 2.6 The Gauntlet *(mastery, optional)* | everything above, together, with a tight par | three boards; the gate's clock runs from the very first tick |

One constraint shapes the whole chapter: **code can't read signposts until
Runes (Chapter 6).** A signpost's numbers are fixed, so the signpost levels
use one board and rules. `squares_ahead()` is the only thing that can differ
between boards, so the multi-board levels come after it.

## Proposed changes to the outline (for approval)

1. **Counting before the guard.** The outline had the guard at 2.3 and
   counting at 2.4. A question guard is only worth having when its answer
   can't be known ahead of time (QA-023). Only `squares_ahead()` can make the
   answer differ between boards, so counting comes first, and the guard asks
   something that differs on every board.
2. **Both signpost rules on both signpost levels.** Each rule alone leaves a
   loophole:
   - "Each number only once" alone: 2.1 could be written with a different
     sum for each stretch, with no variable.
   - "Only signpost numbers" alone: 2.2's distances could be split into
     several moves, with no arithmetic.

   Together, plus a line limit, they close both loopholes.
3. **`squares_ahead()` stops before any gate,** timed ones included, even
   while one is open. The count is then the same whenever you ask, and walking
   that far leaves you right in front of the gate. It also stops before walls,
   the board's edge and signposts.
   - Pits and enemies don't appear in Chapter 2. How it treats them is
     decided when they do (Chapter 4's `look()`).
4. **`pawn.wait(0)` is allowed** and does nothing. A computed wait can come
   out as 0, and it shouldn't be an error. `move(0)` stays an error.
5. **Guards' answers can differ per board.** The level file's other boards
   can override a symbol's legend entry, e.g. give board 2's guard a
   different answer. This is a small engine change.
6. **Counting gems for a guard (the QA-022/023 idea) waits for Chapter 4.**
   The code can't see gems yet: which gems a route passes over is known
   ahead from the map. With `look()` in Chapter 4, it becomes a real count.

## The levels

In every map the pawn starts on `P` at a1, facing north, and `G` is the
goal. `#` is a wall, `S`/`T` are signposts, `X` is a guard's gate, and the
timed gate is `T` in 2.5 and 2.6.

### 2.1 · The Surveyor's Road

- **Trains:** variables: give a number a name, then use the name.
- **Brief:** "The road zigzags up the hill in equal stretches, and the
  signpost says how long each one is. You may write its number only once."
- **Map** (7×7):
  ```
  # # # . . . G
  # # # . # # #
  # # # . # # #
  . . . . # # #
  . # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #
  P S # # # # #
  ```
  - `S`: "Every stretch of this road is 3 squares long. The surveyor was
    most particular about that."
- **Abilities:** `move`, `turn_left`, `turn_right`.
- **Rules:**
  - Numbers must come from the signposts.
  - Each number may appear only once.
  - At most 10 lines of code.
- **Par:** 8.
- **Must fail:**
  - typing the number into every move (breaks "only once")
  - walking one square at a time (over 10 lines)
- **Lesson:**
  - `steps = 2`, then `pawn.move(steps)` twice, with a turn between:
    changing one line changes every move. (Different numbers from the
    level's, so the lesson doesn't hand over the route.)
  - A misspelled name is a `NameError` (a `run error` snippet).

### 2.2 · Doing the Sums

- **Trains:** arithmetic with whole numbers (ints): `+`, `-` and `*`.
- **Brief:** "Two signposts give the distances in pieces. Numbers may only
  come from the signposts, once each, so the sums happen in your code."
- **Map** (7×8):
  ```
  # # # # # # G
  # # # # # # .
  . T # # # # .
  . . . . . . .
  . # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #
  P S # # # # #
  ```
  - `S`: "This hall is 5 squares long. The door out is 1 square before its
    end."
  - `T`: "Beyond the door: 2 rooms to the east, each 3 squares wide. The
    goal is one room north of the last."
- **Abilities:** `move`, `turn_left`, `turn_right`.
- **Rules:**
  - Numbers must come from the signposts.
  - Each number may appear only once.
  - At most 7 lines of code.
- **Par:** 6. (Naming every number is fine style, but it goes over the line
  limit. The limit is what makes arithmetic necessary: without any, the
  route needs at least 8 lines.)
- **Must fail:** typing the distances (they aren't on a signpost).
- **Lesson:**
  - `+`, `-` and `*` on numbers, and on names holding numbers.
  - Multiplication before addition: `2 + 3 * 4` is 14.

### 2.3 · Halfway There

- **Trains:** a function that gives back a value, and whole-number division
  (`//`).
- **Brief:** "Three boards, three different halls. Ride to the end of the
  hall, turn right, and stop halfway along the gallery, rounding down on an
  odd one."
- **New ability:** `pawn.squares_ahead()`. It's the first function in the
  game that gives back a value, and it gets a Codex entry.
- **Boards** (tabs above the board):
  ```
  board 1 (7×6)     board 2 (6×4)    board 3 (8×7)
  # # # # # # #     # # # # # #      # # # # # # # #
  . . . G . . .     . . G . . .      . . . G . . . .
  . # # # # # #     . # # # # #      . # # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #     P # # # # #      . # # # # # # #
  . # # # # # #                      . # # # # # # #
  P # # # # # #                      . # # # # # # #
                                     P # # # # # # #
  ```
  Halls 4, 2 and 5. Galleries 6, 5 and 7: one even, two odd.
- **Abilities:** `move`, `turn_left`, `turn_right`, `squares_ahead`.
- **Rules:** none; the boards do the forcing.
- **Par:** 4.
- **Must fail:**
  - numbers that suit board 1 (wrong on boards 2 and 3)
  - halving with `/`: on board 1, `move()` gets 3.0 and says it needs a
    whole number
- **Lesson:** on a lesson board with a wall ahead:
  - `print(pawn.squares_ahead())`, then storing the count in a name.
  - `7 / 2` is 3.5 but `7 // 2` is 3. `pawn.move(7 / 2)` is a `run error`
    snippet.

### 2.4 · Halt!

- **Trains:** f-strings: putting values into text.
- **Brief:** "A guard blocks the gallery and wants to know how far you've
  come, as a proper sentence. The distance is different on every board."
- **The guard asks:** "Halt! How far have you come? Answer like this: I
  walked 5 squares." The right answer is the hall plus the gallery: 7, 6 and
  8 squares on the three boards.
- **Boards:**
  ```
  board 1 (7×5)     board 2 (8×3)      board 3 (6×7)
  # # # # # # #     # # # # # # # #    # # # # # #
  . . . . . X G     . . . . . . X G    . . . . X G
  . # # # # # #     P # # # # # # #    . # # # # #
  . # # # # # #                        . # # # # #
  P # # # # # #                        . # # # # #
                                       . # # # # #
                                       P # # # # #
  ```
- **Abilities:** `move`, `turn_left`, `turn_right`, `squares_ahead`.
- **Rules:** answer with an f-string. `print("I walked", n, "squares.")`
  would print the same words, so the rule says which tool the level trains.
- **Par:** 7.
- **Must fail:**
  - typing board 1's answer (wrong on the others)
  - answering without an f-string (breaks the rule)
- **Lesson:**
  - `f"I have {n} gems"`: text with a value inside, and sums inside the
    braces.
  - On a lesson board with a question guard: a wrong answer gets the guard's
    reply, then a right one opens the gate.

### 2.5 · Keeping Time

- **Trains:** the remainder operator (`%`): working with cycles.
- **Brief:** "A portcullis rises and falls on a steady beat: open for 2
  ticks, shut for 2. How long to wait depends on how far you've walked."
- **Changed ability:** `pawn.wait()` takes a number of ticks: `wait(3)`,
  `wait(0)`, and `wait()` for 1. Its Codex entry and The Sentry's Round's
  docs change to match.
- **Boards:** a corridor, the gate (`T`, open 2 of every 4 ticks) and the
  goal. The four boards differ only in how far the gate is: 3, 6, 4 and 5
  squares. Each needs a different wait, and one needs none.
  ```
  board 1 (3×6)
  # G #
  # T #
  # . #
  # . #
  # . #
  # P #
  ```
- **Abilities:** `move`, `turn_left`, `turn_right`, `squares_ahead`, `wait`.
- **Rules:** none; the boards do the forcing.
- **Par:** 4.
- **Must fail:**
  - one fixed wait (crushed or bumped on some board)
  - walking straight in
- **Lesson:**
  - `%` as a remainder, and as a clock: `7 % 4` is 3.
  - On a lesson board with a timed gate: `pawn.wait(2)`, one arrival that's
    crushed (`run lost`), and one that gets through.

### 2.6 · The Gauntlet *(mastery, optional)*

- **Trains:** Chapter 2 mastery: counting, sums, f-strings and timing,
  together.
- **Brief:** "Everything at once, on three boards: a hall, a guard who wants
  to know how far you've come, and a portcullis that has kept time since
  your very first move."
- **Board 1** (9×4). Boards 2 and 3 have the same shape with other lengths.
  ```
  # # # # # # # # #
  . . . . X . . T G
  . # # # # # # # #
  P # # # # # # # #
  ```
- **The twist:** the timed gate's clock counts every tick since the start,
  turns included, not just the walk up to it.
- **Abilities:** as 2.5.
- **Rules:** answer the guard with an f-string.
- **Par:** tight, set from the reference.
- **Must fail:**
  - 2.5's approach unchanged: it forgets the ticks before the gate
  - typed answers or waits (wrong on the other boards)

## Engine and UI work

1. **`pawn.squares_ahead()`:** stops as proposed in change 3, costs no tick,
   and gets a Codex entry. It's the first ability that gives back a value.
2. **`pawn.wait(ticks=1)`:** a whole number, 0 or more. Its docstring
   changes, and M3.1.md gets a note.
3. **The two number rules,** checked with `ast`: "numbers must come from the
   signposts" and "each number may appear only once".
   - Numbers inside text don't count. A negative number counts as its
     digits.
   - The Challenge panel words them, e.g. "The only numbers allowed are the
     ones on the signposts: 1, 2, 3 and 5."
4. **Per-board legend entries** for other boards (change 5).
5. **Board tabs** above the board, before and after a run. This was decided
   in M3.md.
6. **Chapter 2 in `chapters.yaml`,** listed before the Testing ground.
7. **Mastery levels:** a `mastery: true` level key, and a "Mastery ·
   optional" badge on the level card and in the Challenge panel.

## Open questions

1. **Mastery and "Next level".** Recommended: 2.5's "Next level" leads to
   the mastery challenge, and the challenge's leads on to Chapter 3.
   Skipping it is always possible from the level list.
2. **A tick counter on the board** (asked in PR #8). Recommended for this
   chapter: during playback, the board shows "Tick 5" beside a timed gate's
   badge. 2.5 and 2.6 are about counting ticks, and seeing the count makes
   the `%` visible.
3. **Titles and signpost wording,** as written above, or changed.

  - the two signpost rules, worded in the Challenge panel
  - per-board legend entries
  - the `mastery` key
  - Other boards are now labelled "board 1", "board 2", ….
- **UI** (`7efe992`):
  - board tabs before and after a run
  - the tick count in a timed gate's badge ("2 of 4 · tick 5"), which
    answers open question 2
  - the "Mastery · optional" tag
- **Levels** (`d9d3e54`): the six levels, their lessons, and blind
  references, wrong attempts and third hints.
  - **Pars:** 8, 6, 3, 7, 4 and 11. Two moved from the provisional
    numbers:
    - 2.3 is 3, not 4: the counts fit inline.
    - 2.6 is 11: it names the ticks so far.
  - **The generator** writes each reference from a skeleton that says no
    more than the brief. The real engine finds the number every hole needs
    on every board. A general expression search then picks the most natural
    expression that fits them all: nothing that goes negative, and no made-up
    numbers when the board shows one. It checks each candidate on extra
    boards the level doesn't ship, so an expression can't fit by
    coincidence.
    - That check caught a mistake in 2.6's third board: its hall was one
      square too long for the guard's answer. The board is fixed.
  - **Every wrong attempt fails as intended:**
    - reused numbers (2.1) and typed distances (2.2) break the rules
    - board 1's numbers bump a wall (2.3), `/` for `//` needs a whole
      number (2.3), and a typed answer breaks the f-string rule (2.4)
    - a fixed wait is bumped or crushed (2.5, 2.6)
- **Changed after play-testing (QA-029, 2026-09-30):** the signposts on 2.1
  and 2.2 told the player nothing the board didn't already show. Their
  numbers were only in the Challenge panel and a hover tooltip. So the
  signpost rules were arbitrary.
  - **2.1** has no signpost. Its one rule is "Your code may contain only one
    number, written once" (the `max_numbers: 1` constraint). It replaces
    both signpost rules, which are gone from the engine.
  - **2.2** now brings in `squares_ahead()` and does its sums on what's
    counted, on three boards: walk up the hall to the door one square
    before its end, then twice as far along the gallery. The numbers can't
    be known ahead of time, so the arithmetic has to be in the code. Par 4.
  - **2.3** is only about `//`: counting was already learned in 2.2.
- **Mastery and "Next level"** (open question 1): the mastery challenge is
  the chapter's last level, so 2.5's "Next level" leads to it.
